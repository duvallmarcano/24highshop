<p><strong>White sage smudges</strong>, known for their smouldering fire, play a crucial role in the traditional ceremonies of the original indigenous peoples of the Americas, including use in sweat lodges. This all-natural incense, consisting of sticks or bunches of white sage, is increasingly appreciated by a wide spectrum of professionals such as doctors, therapists, and body workers, who recognise the potentially beneficial effects of these herbs.</p>
<p>The tradition of smudging - purifying people, places, and objects with smoke - is deeply rooted in history, possibly dating back to the use of campfires in prehistoric times. The practice, which continues today, serves not only as a method of repelling insects, but also has an important spiritual and conservation function. The smoke of certain plants, including white sage, was discovered as a means of preserving food and skins, and offered protection against invisible forces and negative thoughts.</p>
<p>White Sage is specifically known for its power to expel negative energies, such as evil spirits and negative influences, through ceremonial burning. The leaves' unique fragrance not only serves as a natural repellent against moths and mosquitoes, but also plays an important role in various ritual applications. Native Americans in Central America use white sage in various ways during their sweat lodge ceremonies, such as covering the floor, inhaling the smoke, or rubbing sage over the body. These practices are based on the belief that white sage can ward off evil spirits, while good spirits are attracted to the smoke.</p>
<p>The versatility and deep spiritual significance of white sage smudges underscore their enduring value in both traditional and contemporary spiritual practices. By smudging with white sage, people not only connect with ancient traditions and the power of Mother Nature, but also create a space free of negative energies and promote an atmosphere of purity and protection.</p>
