<h2>What is sterilised substrate?</h2>
<p>Substrate is the nutrient medium on which mycelium grows. Professionally sterilising the substrate removes unwanted microorganisms, which significantly reduces the risk of contamination during the first phase of cultivation. As the product is delivered sterile, the grower can decide which genetics to use.</p>
<h2>How does it work?</h2>
<p>Upon receipt, the substrate must first be inoculated with spores or liquid culture. During this process, the substrate is injected with genetic material under sterile conditions. The mycelium then begins to spread through the nutrient-rich medium until complete colonisation is achieved.</p>
<p>Suitable inoculation methods include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Spore syringes containing suspended spores</li>
<li>Liquid culture syringes containing active mycelium</li>
<li>Spore vials in sterile transfer</li>
</ul>
<p>The method chosen may affect the speed and consistency of colonisation.</p>
<h2>How to use sterilised substrate?</h2>
<ol>
<li>Provide a clean and sterile working environment.</li>
<li>Inoculate the substrate with a spore syringe, liquid culture or spore vial.</li>
<li>Place the substrate in a suitable incubation environment.</li>
<li>Wait until the mycelium has completely colonised the substrate.</li>
<li>Then start the fruiting phase under controlled conditions.</li>
</ol>
<p>It is important to note that sterile procedure is essential to avoid contamination during inoculation.</p>
<p><strong>How many ml of spore inoculation:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>
<p><em>Spore syringe</em>: 5 ml for 1200 cc substrate, 10 ml for 2100 cc</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><em>Spore ampoule</em>: half ampoule for 1200 cc, whole ampoule for 2100 cc</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><em>Liquid culture</em>: 2 - 5 ml per litre per 1000 cc</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><em>Homemade liquid culture</em>: first inject 2 ml of spores into the vial, wait 2 weeks, then use the full vial in the substrate.</p>
</li>
</ul>
