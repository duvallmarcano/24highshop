<h2>What is Agar Agar?</h2>
<p>Agar Agar is a natural substance extracted from red algae. In mycology, it is used as a base for culture media because it creates a clean and solid substrate. By adding additional nutrients, such as malt or yeast extract, the medium can be tailored to different culture purposes.</p>
<p>Besides its application in mushroom cultivation, Agar Agar is also known as a plant-based alternative to gelatine. Its sturdy structure and heat resistance make it very suitable for sterile work.</p>
<h2>How does Agar Agar work in mushroom cultivation?</h2>
<p>After heating and cooling, Agar Agar turns into a solid, transparent gel. On this gel, mycelium can visibly expand, allowing you to closely monitor growth, contaminations and structure. This is especially useful for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cloning mushrooms</li>
<li>Testing spores or liquid cultures</li>
<li>Selecting strong mycelium</li>
<li>Research and controlled culture</li>
</ul>
<h2>Use and preparation</h2>
<p>Agar Agar is usually dissolved in water and boiled briefly to create a sterile medium.</p>
<ul>
<li>Use about 15-20 grams of Agar Agar per litre of water</li>
<li>Bring the mixture to the boil</li>
<li>Add malt or yeast extract if required</li>
<li>Pour the hot liquid into sterile petri dishes or jars</li>
<li>Allow to cool to a solid gel</li>
</ul>
<p>After cooling, the medium is ready for inoculation with spores or mycelium.</p>
