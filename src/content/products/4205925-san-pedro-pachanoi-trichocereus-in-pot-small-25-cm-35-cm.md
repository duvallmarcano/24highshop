<h3>General Water Requirements</h3>
<ul>
<li>San Pedro is a desert cactus and needs <strong>very little water</strong>.</li>
<li>Overwatering can cause root rot, especially in humid or cold conditions.</li>
<li>The frequency of watering depends on the season, temperature and humidity.</li>
</ul>
<h3>How Often Do You Water?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Spring &amp; Summer (growing season)</strong>:
<ul>
<li>Water <strong>every 2-3 weeks</strong>, depending on the dryness of the soil.</li>
<li>Let the soil dry completely before watering again.</li>
<li>Use the <strong>"finger test"</strong>: stick your finger 5 cm into the soil - if it is dry, water.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Autumn &amp; Winter (dormant period)</strong>:
<ul>
<li>Reduce watering drastically or stop completely.</li>
<li>Only water a minimal amount if the cactus starts to shrivel.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to Water.</h3>
<ul>
<li>Use <strong>rainwater</strong> or filtered water if possible, as tap water sometimes contains a lot of lime and chlorine, which can affect growth.</li>
<li>Water <strong>directly to the soil</strong>, not over the cactus itself, to avoid fungal problems.</li>
<li>Make sure the pot has drainage holes so that excess water can drain away.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Extra tips</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Check the soil</strong>: San Pedro grows best in an airy, well-draining cactus mix. If necessary, add perlite or sand to reduce water retention.</li>
<li><strong>Indoor or outdoor?</strong> Outdoor cacti dry out faster than indoor plants, so adjust watering accordingly.</li>
<li><strong>Pot material</strong>: Terracotta pots help evaporate excess moisture and prevent root rot better than plastic pots.<br/><br/></li>
</ul>
