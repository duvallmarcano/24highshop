<p>Getting enough protein is important for a healthy body. One option that is becoming increasingly popular is plant-based protein powder. In this blog, we will discuss what plant-based <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555990/">proteins</a> are, their benefits, and how to use them safely and effectively.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>What are Proteins and Amino Acids?</h2>
<p>Proteins are essential building blocks for the body. They are needed to create and maintain tissues, such as muscles, skin, hair and nails. They also play a role in the immune system, hormone production and digestion. Proteins are composed of amino acids, of which there are 20 different types. Nine of these amino acids are considered essential, as the body cannot produce them itself. These must therefore be absorbed through diet.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>Plant versus Animal Proteins</h2>
<p>Animal proteins, such as those found in meat, fish, eggs and dairy, usually contain all the essential amino acids in the right amounts. This makes them 'complete'. However, some plant sources also have a complete amino acid profile, such as soybeans, quinoa and certain mushrooms. While not all plant-based foods are complete, by varying your diet, you can still get all the essential amino acids.</p>
<h3>Good Sources of Plant Protein</h3>
<ul>
<li>Soybeans and products such as tofu and tempeh</li>
<li>Pseudo-cereals such as quinoa and buckwheat</li>
<li>Legumes such as lentils, chickpeas and peas</li>
<li>Specific vegetables such as spinach and broccoli</li>
<li>High-protein mushrooms such as shiitake, which also contain dietary fibre and micronutrients.<br/><br/></li>
</ul>

<h2>Why choose plant-based protein powder?</h2>
<ul>
<li>Vegetarian or vegan alternative: For those who do not consume animal products, plant-based protein is a valuable option.</li>
<li>Environmentally friendly: Plant protein production has a smaller carbon footprint than animal protein production.</li>
<li>Potential health benefits: Studies suggest that replacing animal protein with plant protein may contribute to a reduced risk of cardiovascular disease and improved metabolic processes. However, more research is needed to fully understand these effects.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Plant protein powder: how to use it?</h3>
<p>Plant protein powder can be a convenient way to increase your protein intake, especially if you exercise intensively or struggle to get enough protein through your diet. Some popular types of vegetable protein powder are:</p>
<ul>
<li>Soy protein powder</li>
<li>Pea protein powder</li>
<li>Protein powder from medicinal mushrooms, such as Shiitake and Reishi</li>
</ul>
<p>You can mix these powders into smoothies, soups or sauces. When choosing protein powder, pay attention to quality: go for products without added sugars, artificial flavourings or colourings.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>Medical Claims: Caution Commanded</h2>
<p>Although mushrooms such as <a href="/en/product/3209663-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-health-support-100-grams">Reishi</a> and <a href="/en/product/4204538-shiitake-lentinula-edodes-heart-support-100-grams">Shiitake</a> are often praised for their health benefits because of substances such as <a href="/en/">terpenes</a>, antioxidants and polysaccharides, it is important to know that the purported 'medicinal' properties of these mushrooms are not always sufficiently scientifically substantiated. Consult a doctor or dietician before taking supplements, especially if you have specific health goals or are on medication.</p>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Plant-based protein powder can be a healthy and environmentally friendly way to support your protein intake. By choosing varied and high-quality plant sources, you can meet your need for essential amino acids. Just make sure you choose a reliable product and base your choices on well-researched information.</p>
