<p><strong>What are the best shisha flavours?</strong> Shisha, also known as  <a href="/en/">hookah</a> or waterpipe, has been a popular social tradition for centuries. Smoking shisha is not just about relaxation, the unique flavours are what make the experience truly enjoyable.</p>
<p>Today, there are hundreds of shisha flavours available, ranging from fresh mint to sweet watermelon. In this article, you’ll discover which flavours are global favorites, what they taste like, and what to look for when finding your perfect blend. This way, you can easily find the best shisha flavours for your personal taste.</p>
<p>Shisha smoking originated in the Middle East and India but is now enjoyed all over the world. You’ll need a special  shisha along with tobacco or herbal mixtures. The right flavour ultimately determines how intense, fruity, or refreshing your session will be, and it makes the difference between an ordinary and an exceptional experience.</p>
<h2>The history of shisha tobacco</h2>
<p>Before we discuss the flavours, it’s important to know what shisha tobacco actually is. It’s also called mu’assel and is a blend of tobacco, glycerin, and molasses or honey. Flavourings are often added to it. The molasses creates the thick, sweet smoke typical of shisha, while glycerin makes the clouds denser and fuller. The flavorings vary greatly, from natural fruit extracts to synthetic aromas.</p>
<p>In the past, simple flavours such as honey or pure fruit were used, but with modern techniques and globalization, the possibilities have grown exponentially. Today, countless brands and flavours exist, from timeless classics to unique combinations. Shisha tobacco is delicious, but also has some downsides. Smoking shisha is associated with health risks such as lung cancer, according to this  study by Kadhum et al. (2015). So use shisha in moderation.</p>

<h2>The best classic shisha flavours</h2>
<p>Some flavours are timeless. They were popular when shisha first spread globally — and they still are today. Below are a few of these beloved classics:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Double Apple:</strong> Perhaps the most iconic shisha flavour of all time. It’s a complex mix of sweet apple and strong, anise-like licorice tones. This combination is unique, refreshing, and incredibly satisfying.</li>
<li><strong>Mint:</strong> Mint is an essential flavour in the shisha world. It’s rarely used on its own but almost always mixed to refresh other flavours. A touch of mint can lighten heavy fruit flavours or balance out sweet blends.</li>
<li><strong>Grape:</strong> Another classic that never disappoints. There are several variations — from sweet, dark grape to light and tangy white grape. It’s often combined with mint for a cooling, refreshing effect.</li>
</ul>
<h2>The best new shisha flavours</h2>
<p>In recent years, shisha producers have experimented with more complex and fruity combinations. These modern creations have enriched the shisha world with new, exciting blends. These newer but equally delicious flavours are worth trying:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Blueberry Mint:</strong> One of the most popular fruity flavours. The sweet, slightly tart taste of blueberry mixed with the cool freshness of mint creates a perfectly balanced smoke experience.</li>
<li><strong>Watermelon:</strong> Perfect for sunny days, this flavour delivers a sweet, juicy taste reminiscent of fresh fruit. Watermelon is often blended with mint for an extra refreshing touch, simply delicious!</li>
<li><strong>Orange Cream:</strong> A flavour that combines the creamy softness of vanilla with the sweet, citrusy notes of orange. It’s reminiscent of an orange ice cream, ideal if you enjoy dessert-like flavours.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The science behind flavour perception</h2>
<p>The perception of flavour is a complex process that involves more than just your taste buds. Our sense of smell plays a crucial role in how we perceive taste. Shisha tobacco not only stimulates the palate but also fills your nose with pleasant aromas. This is called orthonasal olfaction — perceiving smells through the nostrils — as explained in this  study by Zhang and Spence (2023).</p>
