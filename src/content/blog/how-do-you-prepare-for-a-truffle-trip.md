<h2>What does preparing for a truffle trip involve?</h2>
<p>Preparing for a truffle trip goes beyond simply deciding how many truffles you want to take. Many experienced users and ceremony facilitators consider three aspects: your body, your mental state and the environment in which the experience takes place.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Physical preparation:</strong> this includes nutrition, rest and avoiding substances.</li>
<li><strong>Mental preparation:</strong> reflecting on your intention, expectations and emotions.</li>
<li><strong>Environment:</strong> a quiet, safe place with as few distractions as possible.</li>
</ul>
<p>It is precisely this combination that is often seen as a key foundation for a pleasant and mindful experience.</p>
<p>If you want to know when it’s sensible to use them and when it isn’t, read our guide on <a href="/en/">when to use and when not to use</a>.</p>
<h2>Why do many people choose to be sober?</h2>
<p>A common piece of advice is not to start a truffle trip on a full stomach. Some people experience less nausea if they eat a light meal a few hours beforehand or fast temporarily.</p>
<p>In addition, many users report that they experience the trip more consciously when it is not preceded by alcohol, a heavy meal or other substances. There is no hard and fast rule that applies to everyone, but many ceremonies deliberately opt for a light meal or an empty stomach prior to the trip.</p>
<h2>Why do some people avoid alcohol, cannabis or other substances?</h2>
<p>Many ceremonies ask participants not to consume alcohol, cannabis or other mind-altering substances beforehand. The reasoning behind this is straightforward and practical: when multiple substances are influencing you at the same time, it becomes harder to recognise exactly what the truffles are doing.</p>
<p>It can also make the experience feel less predictable. Many participants therefore choose to take it easy for a few days beforehand, giving their body and mind the chance to prepare.</p>
<p>Furthermore, extra caution is important if you or anyone in your immediate family has a history of mental health conditions, such as schizophrenia, psychosis or bipolar disorder. </p>
<p>Psychedelics can increase the risk of mental health issues or exacerbate existing ones in people who are susceptible to them. Are you unsure whether a truffle trip is a good idea for you, or are you taking medication? If so, consult a doctor or another qualified healthcare professional beforehand.</p>
<h2>Why do you sometimes hear that sex or masturbation is not recommended?</h2>
<p>This is a much-discussed topic surrounding truffle ceremonies. In some spiritual traditions, it is advised to abstain from sex or masturbation for 24 to 48 hours beforehand.</p>
<p>The idea behind this has not been scientifically proven. It stems mainly from traditions in which sexual energy is seen as something linked to focus, attention and intention.</p>
<p>Not every ceremony follows this guideline. Some participants nevertheless choose to do so consciously, as it helps them approach the ceremony with greater calm and awareness.</p>

<h2>Why is your mindset so important?</h2>
<p>When it comes to psychedelics, you often hear the term<strong>‘set and setting</strong>’. This refers to the fact that your mental state and your surroundings can have a significant influence on how you experience something.</p>
<p>Feelings, expectations, anxiety, trust and the atmosphere around you can all play a part. That is why many people take time beforehand to reflect on questions such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Why do I want to have this experience?</li>
<li>What do I want to explore?</li>
<li>What am I curious about?</li>
<li>Is this the right time for me?</li>
</ul>

<h2>How do you prepare for a truffle trip, step by step?</h2>
<p>Good preparation doesn’t have to be complicated. By consciously paying attention to your body, your mindset and the environment in which you’ll be taking the truffles beforehand, you increase the likelihood of a calm and pleasant experience.</p>
<p>The experience of taking mushrooms is strongly influenced by preparation, environment and intention. If you want to know why these factors make such a difference, read <a href="/en/">‘Setting, intention and preparation</a>’.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Make sure you get plenty of rest.</strong> Try to get a good night’s sleep beforehand and plan your day without any rush or commitments.</li>
<li><strong>Eat lightly.</strong> Many people choose to have a light meal a few hours before taking the truffles or to fast temporarily. This can help to minimise any nausea.</li>
<li><strong>Avoid alcohol and other substances.</strong> By not consuming alcohol, cannabis or other mind-altering substances, the experience remains clearer and you’ll be better able to appreciate the effects of the truffles.</li>
<li><strong>Reflect on your intention.</strong> Ask yourself why you want to have this experience. An intention doesn’t have to be grand or complicated; for many people, curiosity or personal development are valuable starting points in themselves.</li>
<li><strong>Choose a safe environment.</strong> Make sure you have a quiet place where you won’t be disturbed. With a higher dose, many people opt for a trusted trip sitter or professional guidance.</li>
<li><strong>Get everything ready in advance.</strong> Think of water, comfortable clothing, a blanket, perhaps an eye mask, and a playlist of calm music. That way, you won’t have to worry about anything during the experience.</li>
<li><strong>Also give yourself time afterwards.</strong> Don’t plan any appointments or commitments after the trip. Many people find it pleasant to go for a short walk, write or simply relax and reflect afterwards.</li>
</ol>
<p>By following these steps in advance, you’ll lay a solid foundation for a mindful and relaxed truffle experience. Ultimately, every experience is personal, and thorough preparation isn’t a guarantee of a specific outcome – but it is a way to begin the journey with greater focus and calm.</p>
<h2>Journaling and setting intentions</h2>
<p>Many ceremony facilitators recommend putting your thoughts down on paper beforehand. Not because you need to have a perfect goal, but because writing helps you to consciously reflect on what’s going on.</p>
<p>An intention doesn’t have to be grand or complicated. Sometimes a simple sentence is enough, such as: “I’m curious to see what I’ll encounter” or “I want to be present with open attention”.</p>
<p>Some retreats also use meditation, breathwork or preparatory exercises to help participants enter the ceremony in a calmer state.</p>

<h2>What can you expect during a truffle ceremony?</h2>
<p>Every truffle ceremony is different, but there are often a number of standard elements.</p>
<p>If you’d like to gain a better understanding of how different types of experiences and natural substances differ from one another, read about <a href="/en/">the different types of experiences.</a></p>
<h3>Introduction</h3>
<p>Participants get to know one another and the facilitators. This helps to build trust and a sense of safety.</p>
<h3>Sharing intentions</h3>
<p>Anyone who wishes to may share why they are here. This is usually not compulsory, but can help to begin the experience with greater awareness.</p>
<h3>Taking the truffles</h3>
<p>The truffles are ingested, after which the effects gradually set in. The onset may vary from person to person.</p>
<h3>The inner journey</h3>
<p>Music, eye masks and a peaceful environment are often used. The aim is usually to turn one’s attention inwards. Music often plays a greater role in this than people might expect beforehand.</p>

<h2>What if you experience visuals?</h2>
<p>Many people are curious about the visual effects of truffles. Not everyone experiences visuals in the same way. Some people see colours, patterns or images, whilst others mainly notice physical sensations, emotions or thoughts.</p>
<p>Visuals are therefore neither a guarantee nor an indicator of a ‘good’ experience. A truffle trip can be rewarding or intense without any distinct visual effects.</p>
