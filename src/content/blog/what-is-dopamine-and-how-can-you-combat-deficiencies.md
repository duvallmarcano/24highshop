<p>Dopamine is a neurotransmitter, a brain chemical that plays an essential role in mood, sleep, and cognitive functions. It is also a crucial part of the brain's reward system. When dopamine levels get out of balance, it can have negative effects on your mental and physical well-being. In this blog, we explain what dopamine is and give tips on finding natural ways to prevent or reduce dopamine deficiencies.<br/><br/></p>

<h2>What is dopamine?</h2>
<p>Our brains use neurotransmitters, chemical messengers, to transmit signals. These substances are essential for the brain to function properly. <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitters">Neurotransmitters </a>play a role in learning, emotions, sleep and movement, among other things. Dopamine, often called the "feel-good" hormone, is one of these neurotransmitters.</p>
<p>Dopamine is involved in:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Motivation and reward</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Maternal feelings and social connection</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Cognitive functions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Movement</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Reproductive behaviour</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>One of dopamine's best-known functions is its role in the reward system. This system enhances your mood and plays a role in generating happiness hormones, feelings of joy and satisfaction.</p>

<ul>
<li>
<p>Tremors</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Low libido</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Lack of motivation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fatigue and lethargy</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Muscle stiffness</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Not experiencing pleasure in activities you previously enjoyed</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Important note:</strong> Do you have prolonged or severe symptoms? If so, always consult a doctor.</p>
