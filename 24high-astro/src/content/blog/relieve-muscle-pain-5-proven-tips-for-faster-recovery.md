<h2>What is muscle pain?</h2>
<p>All athletes are familiar with muscle soreness after exercise, although you can also suffer from it after a hard day's work. But what exactly is muscle pain? Of course, you then feel sore muscles and, according to researchers, this is probably due to an accumulation of waste products in your body. Experts suggest that lactic acid in particular causes the symptoms. You experience pain and cramping as a result, as well as a stiff feeling in the muscles.</p>
<p>You can also suffer from delayed muscle soreness, or Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS). Professional athletes experience these symptoms, especially at the beginning of the season, according to this 2012 study by Cheung, Hume and Maxwell. When doing exercises for the first time, DOMS can also flare up. The researchers' expectation is that delayed muscle soreness is caused by lactic acid, muscle spasms, connective tissue damage, muscle damage, inflammation and/or the flux of enzymes. A combination of these factors can also lead to DOMS.<br/><br/></p>

<h3>Heat and cold therapy</h3>
</li>
</ol>
<p>This 2021 study by Wang et al looked at the use of heat and cold therapy to relieve muscle pain. It showed that cold therapy had a great effect on the pain level of people with delayed muscle pain as early as within an hour after exercise. It had the best analgesic effect within 24 hours. Heat therapy also had this result, but was also effective longer than 24 hours after exercise. Hot packs were particularly effective in this area.<br /></p>
