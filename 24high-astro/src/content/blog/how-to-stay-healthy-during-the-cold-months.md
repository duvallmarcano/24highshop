<p>Do you notice you feel more tired, cold or less energetic in autumn and winter? You are not the only one: the cold months demand a lot from your body. Less sunlight, lower temperatures and a higher risk of viruses put your resistance to the test. Fortunately, you can do something about it yourself. With the right food, natural support and enough exercise, you can make sure you stay fit, vital and energetic - even when the days get darker.</p>

<p>In this article, you will discover how to prepare your health for the cold months with practical and immediately applicable tips.</p>
<h2>Why pay extra attention to your health in winter?</h2>
<p>In winter, many people feel tired, sick or dejected more quickly. This is not only due to the cold, but also to a lack of sunlight. As a result, your body makes less vitamin D. Moreover, your immune system has to work harder to protect you from viruses. Read more about how sunlight affects your health.</p>
<p>By paying preventive attention to your health, you increase your resistance and reduce the chances of being out of circulation for a long time.</p>
<h2>Tips to get fit through the cold months</h2>
<h3>1. Eat nutritious and seasonal foods</h3>
<p>A strong resistance starts in the kitchen. Make sure you eat a varied diet with plenty of vegetables, fruits, whole grain products and healthy fats.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Seasonal vegetables:</strong> pumpkin, Brussels sprouts and kale are full of vitamins and minerals.</li>
<li><strong>Fruit:</strong> citrus fruits, kiwis and berries are high in <a href="/en/product/1202997-citrus-vitamin-c-together">vitamin C</a>, which supports your immune system. See also this <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5707683/">research by Carr &amp; Maggini (2017)</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Nuts, seeds and fish:</strong> contain zinc and selenium, important minerals for your immune system <a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-Consumer/">(source</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Fatty fish or flaxseed:</strong> provide omega 3 fatty acids, which are anti-inflammatory and good for the cardiovascular system <a href="https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000482">(study</a>).<br/><br/></li>
</ul>
