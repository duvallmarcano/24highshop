<h2>History of the magic mushrooms</h2>
<p>It is likely that the ancestors of the Aztecs and relatives were aware of the psychoactive effects of certain types of magic mushrooms thousands of years ago. This is concluded from the countless mushroom figurines and murals that have been found during archaeological research throughout the area between Central America and Chile. The exact meaning of these finds is still not entirely clear, but it seems that psilocybin mushrooms were mainly used for medical and religious purposes and played a major role in daily life.</p>
<h3>Spanish conquistadors, first European explorers?</h3>
<p>The first written evidence of mushroom consumption comes from the Spanish conquistadors who, when they took up residence in the Americas, recorded the customs and practices of the indigenous people in their logbooks. One of the oldest notes found comes from the 16th-century Spanish monk Bernandino de Sahágun:</p>
<p>‘Before sunrise <a href="/en/">they ate the magic mushrooms</a> with honey, and when they got excited they started dancing, some laughing, some crying. (…) Some sat as if lost in thought. Some saw themselves die; some saw themselves being eaten by a wild beast, others imagined that in battle they captured their enemies, some believed they had committed adultery and that their skulls would be cleaved as punishment.’</p>
<h3>The Mazatec people are one of the first peoples to use magic mushrooms in history?</h3>
<p>The Spaniards did not allow religions other than the Catholic faith, and banned the magic mushroom rituals they believed to be ‘primitive and inhumane’ goods. They even put the death penalty on it. Ethnobotanist Richard Evans Schultes and physicist Plasius Paul Reko discovered in the 1930s that the so-called Veladas, Native American mushrooms ceremonies with both Native American and Catholic spiritual influences, were still held in some areas. However, the American R. Gordon Wasson takes the honor of being the first white person to participate in such a ritual. His quest for ‘the secret of magic mushrooms’ takes him in 1955 to the Oaxaca area in Mexico, the area of ​​the Mazatec people.<br/></p>
<h2>Chemical composition in magic mushrooms</h2>
<p>The main active ingredients of the mushrooms are <a href="/en/">psilocybin and psilocin</a>. The ratio of these substances differs from species to species. The main difference is that psilocin is unstable and breaks down than when you dry the magic mushrooms, while psilocybin can last much longer (a 115-year-old magic mushroom was once found that still contained it). Only psilocin is psychoactive. Psilocybin is converted to psilocin in the liver. The hallucinogenic/visionary effects are roughly equivalent to those of LSD; although psilocybin is 200 times less strong and also has a shorter duration. The effect can last for 4 to 6 hours.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>What is a mushroom mycelium?</h2>
<p>Mycelium is an underground network of cells that has spread all over the tank. The fruit of this mycelium are the magic mushrooms. In nature, <a href="/en/">mycelium</a> form very large networks. In fact, the fungal network in the US state of Oregon is the largest known fungal network in the world. It extends over an area of ​​8.9 square kilometres (1750 football fields).<br/></p>
