<h2>What is a hangover?</h2>

<p>What actually happens in your body when you have a hangover? And where exactly does that hangover come from? Generally, the word 'hangover' is used to describe the brackish state you are in the day or even days after excessive alcohol consumption. A strong hangover can be felt for days after the last drop of alcohol!</p>
<p>How broke you feel will depend partly on how much alcohol you have consumed, but also how fast you drank. Habituation also plays a role. To put it bluntly, a seasoned alcoholic suffers much less from hangovers than someone who lets the occasional party get out of hand. If you are not so fit or have not eaten much, a drink can have a big impact on how you feel the day after. And some people are just unlucky in terms of their tolerance to alcohol.</p>
<h3>Symptoms</h3>
<p>The symptoms of a hangover usually include:</p>
<ul>
<li>headache</li>
<li>nausea, vomiting and intestinal discomfort</li>
<li>fatigue</li>
<li>dehydration</li>
<li>loss of appetite or strong cravings</li>
<li>decreased mood or mood swings</li>
</ul>
<p>The definition of 'hangover', by the way, can also be drawn more broadly. Because not only alcohol can give you a brackish aftertaste, other substances can also have this unwanted effect. Know that a large proportion of your hangovers are caused by lack of sleep and dehydration - in addition to the fact that you have actually consumed toxic substances, of course.<br/><br/></p>
