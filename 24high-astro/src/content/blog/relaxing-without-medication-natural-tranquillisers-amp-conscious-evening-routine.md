<p><strong>Relaxing without medication means working on inner peace through lifestyle, rhythm and possibly natural supplements.</strong> For most people, the biggest gain lies in a consistent evening routine. Herbs such as valerian or ashwagandha can be supportive, but are never the basis.</p>
<blockquote>Those who want to relax without medication first focus on sleep patterns, stimulus reduction and regular evening habits. Natural tranquillisers can be used additionally, if consciously and responsibly.</blockquote>
<h2>What does relaxing without medication mean?</h2>
<p>Relaxing without medication refers to consciously choosing lifestyle interventions instead of prescribed sleep or sedatives. Consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>A regular sleep rhythm</li>
<li>Limiting screen use in the evening</li>
<li>Relaxation rituals such as meditation or breathing exercises</li>
<li>Using herbal teas or supplements as part of a routine</li>
</ul>
<p>According to health authorities, healthy sleep is primarily related to behaviour and consistency, not one specific product<a href="/en/">[1]</a>.</p>
<h2>How do natural tranquillisers work?</h2>

<h3>Valerian (Valeriana officinalis)</h3>
<p>Valerian is traditionally used in herbal teas and supplements. The herb contains substances that can affect GABA receptors in the nervous system - a system involved in relaxation<a href="/en/">[3]</a>.</p>
<p>In Europe, valerian falls under dietary supplements, not medicines.</p>
<h3>Ashwagandha</h3>
<p>Ashwagandha is an adaptogenic herb from the Ayurvedic tradition. Adaptogens are used within wellness routines to support stress balance. The effect is usually subtle and builds with consistent use.</p>
<h3>Passionflower</h3>
<p>Passionflower is often used in evening tea blends. It is known for its mild nature and is often combined with lemon balm or valerian.</p>
<h3>5-HTP</h3>
<p>5-HTP is an endogenous substance involved in serotonin production. Within the EU, no medical claims may be made about action against specific conditions.</p>
<blockquote><strong>Important:</strong> Natural products do not replace medical treatment and work differently for each person.</blockquote>
<h2>Benefits and limitations of natural relaxation</h2>
<h3>Possible benefits</h3>
<ul>
<li>No dependence as with some sleep medications</li>
<li>Fits within broader lifestyle optimisation</li>
<li>Often milder in nature</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
<li>Effect is subtle and not immediately noticeable</li>
<li>Does not work the same for everyone</li>
<li>Can cause interactions with medication</li>
</ul>
<p>Consistency in behaviour has often been shown in research to be more determining than supplement use alone<a href="/en/">[2]</a>.</p>
<h2>Safety and concerns</h2>
<ul>
<li>Always read the label and follow recommended doses.</li>
<li>Do not combine supplements without knowledge of interactions.</li>
<li>Use with medication or medical conditions only in consultation with a doctor.</li>
<li>Do not use during pregnancy or lactation without professional advice.</li>
</ul>
<p>Supplements fall under food legislation and are regulated differently from medicines.</p>
<h2>When should you seek professional help?</h2>
<p>Although lifestyle and natural tranquillisers can support many people, there are situations where professional guidance is important. Do you experience long-term insomnia (longer than a few weeks), severe anxiety symptoms, gloominess or do you notice that stress interferes with your daily functioning? If so, it is wise to contact a GP or qualified healthcare professional. Even if you are already taking medication or are unsure about possible interactions with supplements, expert advice is necessary. Natural support can be valuable, but does not replace medical diagnosis or treatment.</p>
<h2>Which tranquilliser suits whom?</h2>
<p>Not every remedy suits every need. The global guidelines below will help you make an informed choice:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Valerian:</strong> often chosen by people who have particular difficulty falling asleep and need a classic herb to support relaxation in the evening.</li>
<li><strong>Ashwagandha:</strong> more often suits people who experience a lot of stress during the day and want to support their overall stress balance.</li>
<li><strong>Passionflower:</strong> is usually used in mild evening tea combinations for those seeking gentle, natural support.</li>
<li><strong>5-HTP:</strong> is chosen by people interested in supporting their serotonin balance, with extra attention to possible interactions with medication.</li>
</ul>
<p>Remember that the foundation is always a consistent evening routine. Supplements can be supportive, but work best as part of a broader lifestyle strategy.</p>
<h2>Here's how to build an effective evening routine</h2>

<h3>Step 1: Reduce stimuli</h3>
<p>Stop bright screen light at least 60 minutes before bedtime<a href="/en/">[2]</a>.</p>
<h3>Step 2: Fixed relaxation time</h3>
<p>Choose a recurring moment for tea, breathing exercises or journaling.</p>
<h3>Step 3: Optimise your sleep environment</h3>
<ul>
<li>Cool, dark bedroom</li>
<li>Fixed bedtime</li>
<li>No caffeine in the late afternoon</li>
</ul>
