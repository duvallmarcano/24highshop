<p>In cases of unwanted childlessness, the woman is often automatically looked at. Yet in about half of the cases, the cause turns out to be the man. Often this has to do with sperm of reduced quality, such as low sperm count or reduced motility. Besides fertility, sperm count can also be important at other times, for instance when it comes to self-confidence and personal perception. If you notice that your sperm production is not optimal, there is good news: you can take steps yourself to improve it. Adjusting your diet can already make a difference. There are also specific supplements that can support your sperm production. In this article, we discuss what you can do to improve your sperm quality.</p>
<p><strong>Important:</strong> Always consult a doctor or specialist before making any changes to your diet or supplement use, especially if you suspect fertility problems.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>When low sperm quality is a problem</h2>
<p>Fertility is a complex interplay of factors, both in men and women. In cases of unwanted childlessness, the woman is often looked at, while in about half the cases, the cause lies with the man. A common reason is reduced sperm quality, such as low sperm count or sperm of insufficient quality. A healthy sperm <a href="https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/low-sperm-count/symptoms-causes/syc-20374585">count</a> is above 15 million per millilitre of semen, with at least 4% of the sperm cells needing to be normally formed and 32% motile. These characteristics cannot be seen without a medical examination. It is therefore important to consult a doctor when in doubt. A simple semen analysis can provide insight into the quality and quantity of sperm.<br/><br/></p>

<h3>Lifestyle choices:</h3>
<p>Smoking, excessive alcohol consumption and recreational drugs negatively affect sperm.</p>

<h3>Weight:</h3>
<p>Being overweight affects hormone balance, which can negatively affect sperm production.</p>
<h3>Medical conditions:</h3>
<p>Infections (such as STDs), testicular abnormalities and problems with ejaculation can contribute to reduced fertility.</p>
<h3>Environmental factors:</h3>
<p>Exposure to heavy metals, chemicals, high temperatures and radiation can harm sperm.</p>
<h3>Clothing:</h3>
<p>Tight clothing or underwear can increase the temperature around the testicles, which can affect sperm quality.</p>
<h3>Diet:</h3>
<p>A diet low in vegetables, fruits and essential nutrients can lead to a lack of vitamins and minerals important for sperm.<br/><br/></p>

<p>A healthy lifestyle can help improve sperm quality. Here are some practical tips:</p>
