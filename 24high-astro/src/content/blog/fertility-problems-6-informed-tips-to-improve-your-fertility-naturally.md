<p>Did you know that fertility problems affect millions of people worldwide? Whether you've just started trying to conceive or have been on this journey for a while, it can be emotionally and physically challenging. The causes of reduced fertility are often complex, but fortunately, there are natural ways to support your fertility.</p>
<p>According to reliable medical sources like <a href="https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/treating-unexplained-infertility-answers-still-needed-201509248340">Harvard Health Publishing</a>, factors such as nutrition, lifestyle, and supplements can indeed impact your fertility. In this article, we share 6 science-backed tips that may help naturally — for both men and women.<br/><br/></p>

<h2>Tips for Fertility Problems:</h2>
<h2>1. Use Herbs</h2>
<p>These herbs may naturally support your fertility:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ashwagandha:</strong> Oral intake of Ashwagandha may, according to this study, improve sperm count and motility. It also helps regulate reproductive hormone levels.
<p>Note: This effect has mainly been observed in small-scale studies; more extensive human research is needed. Always consult a doctor if you use medication.<br/><br/></p>
</li>
<li><strong>Maca:</strong> According to this study, <a href="/en/product/4211325-maca-powder">Maca </a>may improve sperm quality in men. It might also improve sperm motility in infertile men.
<p>However, results are still inconclusive and the effect size varies across studies. Maca is not a substitute for medical treatment.<br/><br/></p>
</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. Improve Your Diet</h2>
<p>A healthy diet plays a crucial role in fertility. Several studies show that a Mediterranean diet — rich in vegetables, fruits, fish, whole grains, and healthy fats — benefits both male and female fertility. This <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8276703/">study</a> suggests that a healthy diet may indeed help improve fertility in both sexes. Avoid processed foods, trans fats, and excess sugar, as these can increase the risk of insulin resistance and inflammation, which may undermine fertility.</p>
<h2>3. Maintain a Healthy Weight</h2>
<p>Both overweight and underweight can negatively impact fertility. In women, excess weight can lead to insulin resistance and ovulation disorders (e.g., PCOS), while underweight may cause missed periods. According to this <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/obr.12217">study</a>, it's advisable for overweight women considering assisted reproductive technology to lose weight first, which may increase success rates.</p>
<h2>4. Reduce Stress</h2>
<p>Stress disrupts your hormones, which can lower your chances of conception. Chronic stress can even affect the hypothalamus — the brain region responsible for releasing sex hormones. This can reduce sperm production in men and cause irregular ovulation in women. This <a href="https://academic.oup.com/humrep/article-abstract/29/5/1067/2913997">study</a> suggests that stress reduces fertility in women. Techniques like yoga, mindfulness, breathing exercises, and meditation can lower stress levels and potentially boost fertility.<br/><br/></p>

<h2>5. Quit Smoking and Reduce Alcohol Intake</h2>
<p>Smoking has a proven negative effect on fertility, according to these <a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00325481.2015.1015928">researchers</a>. It reduces egg count in women and damages DNA integrity in male sperm. Smoking also likely reduces sperm count. Excessive alcohol intake is also not beneficial to fertility. According to this <a href="https://journals.lww.com/jhrs/fulltext/2012/05020/alcohol_and_fertility.17.aspx">study</a>, alcohol disrupts the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, which regulates hormone release.</p>
<h2>6. Try Supplements for Fertility Problems</h2>
<p>These supplements are mentioned in some studies as potential additions to a healthy diet, though results vary and depend on individual factors.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Zinc:</strong> Zinc is essential for sperm production in men. A deficiency, according to this <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/21/20/7796">study</a>, negatively affects sperm quality and may reduce motility.
<p>Note: Zinc only shows noticeable effects when there is a proven deficiency. Overdosing can be harmful.</p>
</li>
<li><strong>Folic Acid:</strong> Some research suggests folic acid may improve sperm quality in men, although more studies are needed. This study shows that folic acid use increased total normal sperm count and reduced abnormal sperm cells.<br/><br/>Most studies show modest effects, often in combination with other vitamins. More research is required to confirm a causal link.<br/><br/></li>
<li><strong>Omega-3:</strong> These unsaturated fatty acids improve egg quality and sperm functionality. According to this <a href="https://www.cell.com/heliyon/fulltext/S2405-8440(24)05355-6">study</a>, omega-3 use may significantly improve female fertility.
<p>Omega-3 is especially recommended for Western diets, which often lack sufficient fish-derived fats.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Note on Scientific Research:</strong><br/>In this article, we reference several scientific studies describing potential effects of diet, supplements, and lifestyle on fertility. While these studies offer valuable insights, the strength of evidence varies. Some are small-scale, animal-based, or only show correlation rather than causation. Therefore, results cannot always be directly applied to every person or situation.<br/><br/>For a personalized and reliable fertility assessment, we recommend consulting a doctor, gynecologist, or fertility specialist. These professionals can help based on the latest and most clinically relevant information.</p>
