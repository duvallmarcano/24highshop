<h2>What is maca and where does it come from?</h2>
<p>The maca root (Lepidium meyenii) is native to the Andes. It has been found in Peru and surrounding areas since the 14th century. Here it was used as a medicinal plant, but also in food. In fact, maca is one of the few medicinal plants that has a genuinely delicious flavour: a little nutty. It does resemble hazelnut, with a little imagination. Maca root is also used today in <a href="/en/">traditional Eastern medicine</a>.</p>
<p>Maca usually refers to the supplement made from the maca root. This supplement is available as powder and capsules, but it is also incorporated into multivitamins and other healthy products.<br /></p>
<h2>The effects of maca root</h2>
<p>Maca has various effects on your body and mind. It is nicknamed Peruvian ginseng. Those familiar with 'normal' ginseng will know that this plant falls under uplifters. The same seems to be the case for maca. Not much research has been done on it yet, but some studies among mice and women suggest that it can help combat fatigue. In a <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27548190/">2016 study</a>, nearly 200 participants took 3 grams of maca powder every day for 12 weeks, which improved both their mood and energy.</p>
<p>There are some studies that point to other effects. For instance, it is possibly an anti-inflammatory and may give your brain a helping hand. Another interesting result is that it can help women suffering from <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6419242/">menopause</a>; it can relieve various symptoms.</p>
