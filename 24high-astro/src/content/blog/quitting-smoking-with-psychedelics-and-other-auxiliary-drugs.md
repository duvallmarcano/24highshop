<h2>Why is quitting smoking so difficult?</h2>
<p>Smoking tobacco is a common habit, but it is also one of the most difficult addictions to overcome. Smoking <a href="https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tobacco">kills</a> 8 million people every year, including 1.3 million non-smokers who die from secondhand smoke. Ultimately, half of all smokers die as a result of smoking. Yet a large proportion of the world's population still smokes.</p>
<p>Nicotine activates the brain's reward system and temporarily releases dopamine. This creates a feeling of relaxation and reward while also causing strong dependence. As a result, many people are unable to quit permanently without support.</p>
<h2>Which tools increase the chances of quitting successfully?</h2>
<p>Research on smoking cessation clearly shows that the chances of success increase when support tools are used. These range from medication and therapy to nicotine replacement products such as patches, chewing gum, lozenges, and sprays.</p>
<p>Nicotine replacement products are known to reduce cigarette cravings. Even more effective appears to be the <a href="https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/using-both-nicotine-patches-and-gum-together-improves-the-chances-of-quitting-smoking/">combination</a> of a nicotine patch together with a faster-acting nicotine product, such as nicotine gum. A study involving more than 40,000 smokers showed that this combination can significantly increase the likelihood of successfully quitting smoking.</p>

<h2>Can ketamine help with quitting smoking?</h2>
<p>Researchers are currently investigating the effectiveness of ketamine as an aid for smoking cessation. Previous studies have already shown that ketamine may have therapeutic applications for depression and anxiety disorders. This has also sparked interest in its potential role in addiction treatment.</p>
<p>How does it work? It appears to be related to the brain's reward system. Smoking triggers the release of dopamine, making nicotine use feel rewarding. Ketamine may influence the <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00277/full">receptors</a> involved in this process, which may help reduce cravings.</p>
<p>Researchers are also studying similar effects in other addictions, including alcohol, cocaine, cannabis, and opioid addiction.</p>

<h2>Can psilocybin help with quitting smoking?</h2>
<p>Like ketamine, psilocybin is also known for its potential therapeutic applications. Psilocybin is the active compound found in <a href="/en/">magic mushrooms</a> and magic truffles. At higher doses it produces psychedelic effects, while some people choose to microdose with much smaller amounts.</p>
<p>Research suggests that psilocybin may contribute to behavioural change and help break deeply ingrained patterns. <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27441452/">Studies</a> have shown promising results in programmes where psilocybin is combined with professional guidance for people who want to quit smoking.</p>
<p>Although further research is still needed, these findings are among the most interesting developments in addiction treatment in recent years.</p>
<h2>Which natural remedies may provide support?</h2>
<h3>Yoga and meditation</h3>
<p>Did you know that yoga, meditation, and relaxation exercises may help reduce nicotine cravings? For many people, stress is a major trigger for smoking. By practising meditation or yoga regularly, some people learn to cope better with stress and withdrawal symptoms.</p>

<h3>Tobacco substitutes such as damiana</h3>
<p>Not every tobacco smoker is heavily addicted to nicotine. Some people mainly use tobacco while smoking a joint or in social situations. In such cases, a natural tobacco substitute such as <a href="/en/product/8101867-damiana-indian-elements">damiana</a> may be an alternative.</p>
<p>Damiana contains no nicotine and is not physically addictive like tobacco. However, it is still wise to be moderate with anything that is smoked, as smoke can place strain on the lungs.</p>

<h3>Passionflower</h3>
<p><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30707857/">Several</a> <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22873194/">studies</a> on passionflower, also known as <a href="/en/product/8101630-passion-flower-indian-elements">Passiflora incarnata</a>, suggest that this plant may help support people dealing with stress, anxiety, and sleep problems.</p>
<p>Because many people experience restlessness and sleeping difficulties while quitting smoking, passionflower is sometimes used as part of a broader smoking cessation strategy. In addition, the plant is well known for its relaxing properties.</p>
<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<p>The most effective approach often combines motivation, behavioural support, and, where appropriate, evidence-based stop-smoking aids such as nicotine replacement therapy or prescribed medication. The best method varies from person to person, but having a clear quit plan and support greatly improves the chances of long-term success.</p>

<p>Early scientific research suggests that psilocybin-assisted therapy may have potential for helping some people with nicotine addiction. However, this approach is still being studied and is not an approved treatment in most countries. Psilocybin should not be considered a proven or stand-alone method for quitting smoking.</p>

<p>Ketamine has been investigated in limited research for several mental health conditions and addictions, but it is not an established treatment for nicotine addiction. More research is needed before its effectiveness and safety for smoking cessation can be determined.</p>

<p>Some people find that natural approaches such as mindfulness, regular exercise, breathing techniques, or herbal teas help them manage cravings and stress while quitting. Although these methods may provide support, they generally work best alongside evidence-based smoking cessation strategies rather than as a replacement.</p>
