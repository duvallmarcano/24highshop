<p>CBD and THC are both active compounds from the cannabis plant that have gained significant attention in recent years—both in healthcare and among consumers. Although they are often mentioned together, they differ fundamentally in their effects, legal status, and applications.</p>
<p>This article offers a science-based comparison between CBD and THC to help you make more informed decisions when using cannabis-related products.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>What Are Cannabinoids, and How Do They Work?</h2>
<p>The cannabis plant (<em>Cannabis sativa L.</em>) contains over 100 bioactive substances, known as cannabinoids. The two most well-known are:</p>
<ul>
<li>THC (<em>delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol</em>)</li>
<li>CBD (<em>cannabidiol</em>)</li>
</ul>
<p>Both compounds interact with the endocannabinoid system—a complex receptor network involved in pain perception, mood, appetite, and sleep regulation.</p>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms19030833">Zou &amp; Kumar (2018), International Journal of Molecular Sciences</a></p>

<h2>CBD Is Non-Psychoactive, THC Is Not</h2>
<p>The primary distinction between the two lies in their effect on the brain:</p>
<ul>
<li>THC activates CB1 receptors in the brain, leading to a "high" feeling and affecting memory, coordination, and perception.</li>
<li>CBD is non-psychoactive and may even mitigate THC’s effects. Many users report relaxation without altered consciousness.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Study:</strong> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s13311-015-0387-1">Blessing et al. (2015), Neurotherapeutics</a><br/><br/></p>

<h2>Different Plant Sources</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>CBD</strong> is extracted from hemp, which naturally contains high CBD and low THC.</li>
<li><strong>THC</strong> is derived from marijuana, selectively bred for high THC content.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Applications and Use</h2>
<h3>CBD: General Wellness</h3>
<p>CBD is widely used as a supplement and available in oils, capsules, teas, creams, and skincare. Potential uses include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stress and anxiety relief</li>
<li>Improved sleep</li>
<li>Muscle and joint support</li>
<li>Skin issues (e.g., eczema or acne)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> Most health claims are not yet approved by the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA).</p>
<h3>THC: Medicinal Use Only</h3>
<p>THC is available by prescription and may be used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Chronic pain management</li>
<li>Muscle spasms (e.g., MS)</li>
<li>Nausea due to chemotherapy</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659074/">Pubmed</a></p>
