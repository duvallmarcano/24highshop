<p>When you delve into psychedelics, you quickly come across the terms set and setting. You may have heard of them before, but not yet know exactly what is meant by them. Yet these two factors are hugely important for your experience. In this blog, you will read what set and setting mean, why they determine your trip, and how you can use them to your advantage.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>What do set and setting mean?</h2>
<p>Psychedelics like <a href="/en/shop/mushrooms/growkits-mushroom">magic mushrooms,</a> <a href="/en/shop/mushrooms/magic-truffles">truffles</a> or LSD can have a profound effect on your perception, mood and consciousness. This makes them powerful - but also vulnerable. The terms<strong> </strong>set (your inner state) and setting (your environment) were introduced in the 1960s by US psychologist and psychedelic researcher Timothy Leary. He argued that these two factors help determine whether a trip becomes positive, instructive or overwhelming.</p>
<h3>The 'set': how do you feel about yourself?</h3>
<p>The set refers to your mental and emotional state before you start tripping. Are you relaxed, curious and balanced? Then you are more likely to have a positive experience. On the other hand, if you feel stressed, anxious or sad, those feelings may be magnified during tripping. After all, psychedelics amplify what is already inside you.</p>
<p>So it's wise to take an honest look at yourself: do you feel at ease? Are you comfortable in your own skin? If not, it may be better to postpone your trip. Take time to prepare yourself mentally, possibly with breathing exercises, meditation or a good conversation with someone you trust.</p>
<h3>The 'setting': where and with whom will you be tripping?</h3>
<p>The setting is the physical and social environment in which you will be tripping. A safe, comfortable and quiet place contributes to a pleasant experience. Your own home is often a good choice, especially if you have little experience. A familiar place in nature - provided you are not disturbed - can also be a fine setting.</p>
<p>Also pay attention to who you are with. Are they people you know well and trust? Fine. A so-called <em>trip sitter</em> (someone who stays sober and keeps an eye on you) can provide extra safety. Avoid crowded, unfamiliar or chaotic environments - these can feel overstimulating or confusing under the influence of psychedelics.<br/><br/></p>

<h2>How to prepare set and setting properly?</h2>
<p>Preparation is crucial. Here are some practical tips to optimise your set and setting:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Choose a calm and familiar environment</strong>: Provide soft lighting, comfortable sitting or lying corners, soothing music and minimal distractions.</li>
<li><strong>Ensure emotional stability</strong>: Avoid tripping if you are gloomy, anxious or overtired. Only do it when you really feel ready.</li>
<li><strong>Never go tripping under pressure</strong>: Don't let yourself be persuaded or rushed. Your own intention and motivation are leading.</li>
<li><strong>Consider counselling</strong>: Having a sober, calm person around can help if the trip gets intense.<br/><br/></li>
</ul>

<h2>Beware of mental health problems or medication</h2>
<p>If you are dealing with psychological complaints such as depression, anxiety disorders or psychosis sensitivity, it is strongly advised against experimenting with psychedelics. Research - as summarised in <a href="https://www.healthline.com/health/depression/does-depression-lead-to-substance-abuse">Healthline</a> and at the <a href="https://www.scripps.edu/newsandviews/e_20030929/parsons">Scripps Research Institute</a> - shows that people with these symptoms are more susceptible to negative effects or addiction.</p>
<p>Among other things, psychedelics affect the serotonin systems in your brain, which poses a risk if you are already vulnerable. In that case, consider talking to a psychologist or doctor before you decide to trip.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>Finally: follow your gut</h2>
<p>Set and setting are no guarantee of a perfect trip, but they make a world of difference. Trust your gut: if it doesn't feel right, postpone your trip. Your mental and physical safety always comes first.</p>
<p>Do you have questions about your mental health or substance use? Then contact a doctor, therapist or an organisation such as <a href="https://www.jellinek.nl">Jellinek</a> or the <a href="https://www.trimbos.nl">Trimbos</a> Institute for reliable information and help.</p>
