<h3>Correctly watering Peyote cactus</h3>
<p>Correctly watering Peyote (Lophophora Williamsii) in a pot is essential for healthy growth and preventing root rot. Here is a detailed guide:</p>
<h3>1. Water frequency</h3>
<ul>
<li>Spring &amp; Summer: Water once every 2-3 weeks, depending on temperature and humidity. Wait until the soil is completely dry before watering again.</li>
<li>Autumn: Reduce frequency to once a month.</li>
<li>Winter (dormant period): Stop watering almost completely unless the cactus starts to shrivel. Then water a very small amount (a few drops per month).</li>
</ul>
<h3>2. How much water do you give?</h3>
<ul>
<li>Use demineralised or rainwater if possible, as tap water can contain lime that is harmful to the Peyote.</li>
<li>Water directly to the soil and avoid watering on the cactus itself, as this can cause rot.</li>
<li>Use the "soak and dry" method: pour water until it starts leaking from the bottom of the pot, and let the soil dry completely before watering again.</li>
</ul>
<h3>3. Climate and extra tips</h3>
<ul>
<li>Put the Peyote in half-sun to indirect light, as too much sun can cause burns.</li>
<li>If the humidity is high, reduce watering even further.</li>
<li>Watch for signs of overwatering: soft or yellow parts and a spongy feel of the cactus indicate root rot.</li>
</ul>
