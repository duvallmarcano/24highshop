<p class="text-center"><img alt="CAN CBD WORK AS A PAIN KILLER?" height="333" src="../../../images/image.php@n=77-1750087304.jpg&amp;f=news&amp;w=500&amp;h=500" style="width: 500px; max-width: 100%; max-height: 500px; height: auto;" width="500"/></p><div style="padding: 0 10px;"><p>CBD (cannabidiol) is being investigated as a possible additional treatment for pain complaints. Chronic pain is often treated with regular painkillers, which can be effective, but also have side effects. This blog discusses the current scientific insights into the role of CBD in pain management.</p>
<p><em>This blog is intended as a neutral summary of scientific insights into CBD and pain management. No products are promoted. The sources used are as independent and peer-reviewed as possible.</em></p>
<p><br/><img alt="Graph showing the effect of CBD on pain signals" src="../../files/index.html@id=2618.jpg&amp;file=cbd+and+pain.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><br/></img></p>
<h2>How CBD works</h2>
<p>CBD (cannabidiol) is one of the many <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="../137/whats-the-difference-between-the-cannabinoid-thc-and-cbd.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;"><span>cannabinoids</span></a></span> that occur in the hemp plant. Other well-known cannabinoids include CBG, CBC and THC. Unlike THC, CBD has no psychoactive effect. CBD appears to influence certain processes in the body via the endocannabinoid system (ECS), including pain perception, mood and inflammatory responses.</p>
<p>The ECS plays a role in maintaining balance in the body. CBD can bind to receptors within this system, which may lead to a changed sensitivity to pain stimuli. In this way, CBD can contribute to the modulation of pain signals.<br/><br/><img alt="Bottle of CBD oil used for chronic pain" src="../../files/index.html@id=973.jpg&amp;file=CBD-OLIECANNABIS.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><br/></img></p>
<h2>Dosage forms of CBD</h2>
<p>CBD is available in various forms, including oil, capsules, crystals, hash and edibles. CBD oil is popular because of its easy administration under the tongue, which ensures relatively fast absorption. The most suitable form depends on personal preference, ease of use and medical conditions.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>CBD for migraine?</h2>
<p>There is still limited clinical research available on the use of CBD for migraine. Preliminary indications suggest that CBD may relieve muscle and joint pain that sometimes accompanies migraine attacks. There is also a hypothesis about a link between migraines and a deficiency of the body's own cannabinoids, a condition called Clinical Endocannabinoid Deficiency (<span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;">CECD</span>).</p>
<p>Dr. Ethan Russo, affiliated with the International Cannabis and Cannabinoids Institute, has written papers on this subject. In <span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28861491/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">a 2016 study</a></span> examined cerebrospinal fluid from migraine patients. The results showed abnormalities in levels of anandamide, a neurotransmitter that influences pain and mood. While these findings are interesting, additional research is needed to confirm a direct link between CBD and migraine treatment.<br/><br/><img alt="Visualization of endocannabinoid system and CBD interaction" src="../../files/index.html@id=1989.jpg&amp;file=cbdflesje.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/><br/><br/></p>
<h2>CBD in arthritis</h2>
<p>In a <span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4851925/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">preclinical research</a></span> from 2016 examined the effects of CBD in rats with arthritis. The animals were given topical CBD gel at various dosages. The results showed that joint swelling decreased and pain scores improved, with no side effects reported.</p>
<p>The researchers concluded that CBD has potential as an anti-inflammatory and analgesic agent for conditions such as arthritis. It is important to note that this study was conducted in animals; confirmation in humans requires additional clinical research.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>Considerations for Using CBD</h2>
<p>While some studies suggest that CBD may have positive effects on pain, its effectiveness has not been proven for all types of pain. The side effects of CBD seem to be limited and mild (such as fatigue and decreased appetite), but interactions with other medications are possible.</p>
<p>Are you considering using CBD as a complementary treatment? Always consult a doctor first, especially if you are taking medication or are under medical treatment. CBD can affect the effects of certain medications.l;nfluences.</p>
<h3>Scientific evidence</h3>
<p>Several studies have looked at the effect of CBD on pain relief. An animal study published in the <em>European Journal of Pain</em> (Hammell et al., 2016) showed that topical application of CBD gel could reduce inflammation and pain in rats with arthritis [<span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4851925/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span>].</p>
<p>In addition, a review study by Dr. Ethan Russo (2016) noted a possible link between endocannabinoid deficiencies and conditions such as migraines [<span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28861491/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span>].</p>
<p>A 2024 systematic review published in the journal <em>Pharmaceuticals</em> examined both animal and human studies on the use of CBD for pain management:<br/><br/><em>“Based on the review conducted, there is sufficient clinical and preclinical evidence that CBD may be an effective and safe treatment for reducing pain, due to its analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties. These effects appear to be primarily mediated through activation of TRPV-1, 5HT-1A, and CB1 receptors. CBD has shown particular potential in conditions such as osteoarthritis and chronic pain. However, it is emphasized that current clinical evidence is still limited and that more studies are needed with isolated CBD.” </em>— Cásedas et al., 2024, <em>Pharmaceuticals</em><br/><span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8247/17/1/76" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Link to source (English)</a></span></p>
