<h2>What are alkaloids?</h2>
<p>Alkaloids are found in all kinds of plants and animals. These substances can have various effects, from psychoactive to toxic. Some poison frogs also contain this substance. A few alkaloid species are highly toxic and can be lethal even in small doses.</p>
<p>There are different types of alkaloid compounds. For instance, some help transmit brain signals, such as the happiness substance <span style="color: #2c13df;"><a href="../253/what-are-happiness-hormones-and-how-do-they-affect-your-mood.html" style="color: #2c13df; text-decoration: underline;">serotonin</a></span>.<br/><br/></p>
<h2>Alkaloids as medicine</h2>
<p>For centuries, alkaloids have been used to make medicines. Some alkaloids are strong painkillers, such as morphine and codeine. These two substances are both extracted from <span style="color: #2c13df;"><a href="../../article/0201212-opium-poppy-papaver-somniferum-5-gram.html" style="color: #2c13df; text-decoration: underline;">poppy</a></span>, which is also the source of opium. Other alkaloids, such as quinine from tree bark, were formerly used against malaria. In modern medicine, alkaloids are used to fight infections and autoimmune disorders.<br/><br/><img src="../../files/index.html@id=2164.jpg&amp;file=magic-paddo_s.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2>Tripping and getting high: the best-known psychoactive alkaloids</h2>
<p>Many alkaloids cause you to get high, energise or trip. From your daily cups of coffee and tea to those lines at the weekend and your microdose protocol. In all of these, the active ingredient is an alkaloid.</p>
<h3>Caffeine</h3>
<p>The best-known alkaloid is caffeine. You can find caffeine in coffee, tea, chocolate and energy drinks. Caffeine stimulates the central nervous system, which can lead to increased alertness, concentration and energy levels.</p>
<h3>Cocaine</h3>
<p>Cocaine is an alkaloid. We now know it purely as a recreational drug, but did you know that it was once developed as a drug? In 1884, it was marketed as a local anaesthetic. Something you can't imagine now....</p>

<h3>Cathinone: qat or khat</h3>
<p><span style="color: #2c13df;"><a href="../343/chewing-for-energy-all-about-the-effects-and-uses-of-qat.html" style="color: #2c13df; text-decoration: underline;">Qat</a></span> is not well known to us, but in Africa and some Arab countries it is an important source of energy. Other effects include euphoria, feeling more like socialising and appetite suppression. So in that respect, it is quite similar to other well-known pep drugs. Qat (a.k.a. khat) owes its uplifting effects and its name to the alkaloid cathinone. You use this energy booster purely naturally; you chew the leaves, swallow the released juice and eventually spit out the remnants of the leaves. You can also make tea from the dried leaves.</p>
