<h2>What exactly is strength training?</h2>
<p>Strength training is a form of exercise where you challenge your muscles to become stronger. This can be done with weights, resistance bands, or your own body weight. Unlike endurance training (such as running or cycling), strength training focuses on building muscle mass and strength.</p>
<p>By training in a varied way, you can strengthen different muscle groups and develop your body evenly. Strength training is suitable for everyone, regardless of age or gender, and has more to offer than just muscle growth.</p>
<p><img alt="Physical benefits of strength training" src="../../files/index.html@id=2703.jpg&amp;file=fffq.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2>Physical benefits of strength training</h2>
<h3>Why strength training is crucial after your 30s</h3>
<p>From the age of thirty, muscle mass gradually declines. This process, called sarcopenia, can be slowed down by doing strength training regularly. This way you maintain strength, stability, and mobility well into old age.</p>
<h3>Reduced risk of type 2 diabetes</h3>
<p>Strength training improves insulin sensitivity and contributes to stable blood sugar levels. A systematic review in<span> </span><em>Diabetes Care</em><span> </span>(2011) concluded that strength training is effective in improving glycemic control in people with type 2 diabetes.<span> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/34/5/943/27399" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span></p>
<h3>Better posture and fewer complaints</h3>
<p>By training your core (abs, back, and shoulders), you develop stronger posture. This helps prevent back and neck problems. A publication in<span> </span><em>Sports Health</em><span> </span>(2017) emphasized that targeted strength training can reduce back problems.<span> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5436414/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span></p>
<h3>Positive influence on life expectancy</h3>
<p>Muscle strength and muscle mass are linked in research to a lower risk of premature death. This is partly due to the positive effect on metabolic health, weight, and blood pressure.</p>
<h3>Higher energy expenditure at rest</h3>
<p>Muscles use more energy than fat, even at rest. More muscle mass increases your resting metabolism, which supports weight management.</p>
<p><img alt="Mental benefits of strength training" src="../../files/index.html@id=2702.jpg&amp;file=dw.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2>Mental benefits of strength training</h2>
<h3>Cognitive benefits with aging</h3>
<p>Strength training protects the brain. In a study published in<span> </span><em>NeuroImage: Clinical</em><span> </span>(2020), older adults at increased risk of Alzheimer’s trained with weights for six months. A year later, cognitive benefits were still visible, partly thanks to structural changes in the hippocampus.<span> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213158220300206" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span></p>
<h3>A natural mood booster</h3>
<p>During strength training, your body produces endorphins: a neurotransmitter that improves mood. A meta-analysis in<span> </span><em>JAMA Psychiatry</em><span> </span>(2018) showed that resistance training can reduce depressive symptoms, even in people without a depression diagnosis.<span> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2680311" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span></p>
<h3>Better sleep</h3>
<p>A review in<span> </span><em>Sleep Medicine Reviews</em><span> </span>(2019) concluded that resistance training can contribute to better sleep quality and faster sleep onset.<span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"></span></p>
<p><img alt="Supplements that can support strength training" src="../../files/index.html@id=2707.png&amp;file=cdd.png" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2>Supplements that can support strength training</h2>
<p>Supplements are not always necessary but can be useful during intensive training periods or if you have a deficiency. If in doubt, get tested by a doctor.</p>
<h3>Magnesium</h3>
<p>This mineral plays a role in both muscle contraction and relaxation. During physical exertion, your need for magnesium increases. It can be found in leafy greens, legumes, nuts, and dairy.</p>
<h3>Proteins</h3>
<p>Proteins are essential for muscle building and recovery. Make sure you get enough protein from food: think dairy, eggs, legumes, nuts, and meat. With high training intensity, a protein supplement can be helpful.</p>
<h3>Chaga</h3>
<p>This medicinal mushroom has traditionally been used to support physical resilience. A mouse study published in<span> </span><em>Phytotherapy Research</em><span> </span>(2010) suggests that Chaga extract may reduce fatigue symptoms by lowering lactate levels.<span> </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20486249/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Source</a></span><br/><br/></p>
<h2>Want to start strength training?</h2>
<p>Strength training can be a valuable investment in your health. Start slowly, train with variety, and consult a specialist if you have physical complaints.</p>
