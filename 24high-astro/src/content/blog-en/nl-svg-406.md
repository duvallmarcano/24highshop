<p class="text-center"><img alt="HOW TO STAY HEALTHY DURING THE COLD MONTHS?" height="333" src="../../../../../../images/image.php@n=467-1759151105.jpg&amp;f=news&amp;w=500&amp;h=500" style="width: 500px; max-width: 100%; max-height: 500px; height: auto;" width="500"/></p><div style="padding: 0 10px;"><p>Do you notice you feel more tired, cold or less energetic in autumn and winter? You are not the only one: the cold months demand a lot from your body. Less sunlight, lower temperatures and a higher risk of viruses put your resistance to the test. Fortunately, you can do something about it yourself. With the right food, natural support and enough exercise, you can make sure you stay fit, vital and energetic - even when the days get darker.</p>
<article>
<article>
<p>In this article, you will discover how to prepare your health for the cold months with practical and immediately applicable tips.</p>
<h2>Why pay extra attention to your health in winter?</h2>
<p>In winter, many people feel tired, sick or dejected more quickly. This is not only due to the cold, but also to a lack of sunlight. As a result, your body makes less vitamin D. Moreover, your immune system has to work harder to protect you from viruses. Read more about how sunlight affects your health.</p>
<p>By paying preventive attention to your health, you increase your resistance and reduce the chances of being out of circulation for a long time.</p>
<h2>Tips to get fit through the cold months</h2>
<h3>1. Eat nutritious and seasonal foods</h3>
<p>A strong resistance starts in the kitchen. Make sure you eat a varied diet with plenty of vegetables, fruits, whole grain products and healthy fats.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Seasonal vegetables:</strong> pumpkin, Brussels sprouts and kale are full of vitamins and minerals.</li>
<li><strong>Fruit:</strong> citrus fruits, kiwis and berries are high in <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../article/1202997-citrus-vitamin-c-together.html" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">vitamin C</a></span>, which supports your immune system. See also this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5707683/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">research by Carr &amp; Maggini (2017)</a></span>.</li>
<li><strong>Nuts, seeds and fish:</strong> contain zinc and selenium, important minerals for your immune system <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-Consumer/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(source</a></span>).</li>
<li><strong>Fatty fish or flaxseed:</strong> provide omega 3 fatty acids, which are anti-inflammatory and good for the cardiovascular system <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000482" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(study</a></span>).<br/><br/></li>
</ul>
<img alt="healthy food important for winter" src="../../../../../files/index.html@id=2741.jpg&amp;file=healthyfoiod.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><br/>
<h3>2. Make careful use of supportive herbs</h3>
<p>Certain herbs have been used for centuries to support the immune system. Although some studies suggest positive effects, the scientific evidence is not always unequivocal. In addition, the use of herbs can vary from person to person and is not without risks, especially with chronic conditions or medication use. Therefore, always consult a doctor or qualified healthcare professional before using herbs as a supplement.</p>
<p><strong>Some herbs regularly studied in the context of resistance:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ginger:</strong> Often used for its anti-inflammatory properties and support for digestive complaints. There is some evidence for these effects, but results vary between studies <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3665023/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(Mashhadi et al.</a></span>).</li>
<li><strong>Echinacea:</strong> Some studies suggest that echinacea may possibly reduce the duration and severity of colds, but other studies show no significant effect <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058675/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(Hudson &amp; Vimalanathan</a></span>).</li>
<li><strong>Garlic:</strong> Known for its antimicrobial properties. Some studies suggest a possible protective effect against colds, but this has not been universally confirmed <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11697022/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(source</a></span>).</li>
<li><strong>Turmeric:</strong> Contains <span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../article/1202911-curcumin-turmeric-complex-together.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">curcumin</a></span>, a substance with possible anti-inflammatory properties. There are positive findings, but the bioavailability of curcumin is low, which affects its effectiveness <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664031/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(study</a></span>).</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Important:</strong> Natural remedies are not necessarily safe or suitable for everyone. Always discuss the use of herbs with an expert, especially if you are on medication, pregnant or have health problems.</p>
<h3>3. Consider supplements as a supplement</h3>
<p>Not everyone gets everything through food alone in winter. Supplements can be a useful addition, especially with:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Vitamin D:</strong> important for bones and immune system. Extra intake is often needed in winter.</li>
<li><strong>vitamin C:</strong> supports your resistance and helps with iron absorption <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5707683/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">(research</a></span>).</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3:</strong> contributes to heart health and can positively influence inflammatory processes in the body.</li>
<li><strong>Zinc:</strong> plays a role in many body processes and can shorten a cold.</li>
</ul>
<h3>4. Keep moving</h3>
<p>Even if it is tempting to stay indoors: stay active! Exercise stimulates your circulation, reduces stress and strengthens muscles and bones. Even a daily walk outside has a positive effect. Moreover, daylight helps regulate your biological clock and mood.</p>
<p><img alt="winter and sleep for better health" src="../../../../../files/index.html@id=2740.jpg&amp;file=wintertimes.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><br/></img></p>
<h2>Also pay attention to sleep, stress and hygiene</h2>
<p>Staying healthy in winter is not just about what you eat or how much you exercise. Your daily habits play at least as big a role. Think about sleeping well, keeping stress under control and simple hygiene measures. These may not be exciting tips, but they definitely make a difference to your resistance.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sleep:</strong> A good night's sleep is essential for a strong immune system. Aim for 7 to 9 hours of sleep per night and maintain a regular rhythm. Avoid screen use right before bed and make sure your bedroom is cool and quiet.</li>
<li><strong>Reduce stress:</strong> Chronic stress weakens your defences. Relaxation exercises, breathing techniques and time for yourself will help you feel more balanced.</li>
<li><strong>Hygiene:</strong> Regular handwashing, good ventilation and keeping surfaces clean reduces the risk of viruses, especially in the colder months when we are indoors more often.</li>
</ul>
<p>By also paying attention to sleep, stress and hygiene, you naturally support your body. It takes little effort, but will help you get through the cold months more energetic and healthier.</p>
