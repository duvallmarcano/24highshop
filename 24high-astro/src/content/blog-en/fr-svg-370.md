In principle, it is not harmful, but we completely understand if you find it quite annoying anyway. After all, if you fret all day, it can be detrimental to your <span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../healthshop/228-sleep.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">sleep</a></span><span> and </span><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../healthshop/227-focus-amp-concentration.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">concentration</a></span>. Fortunately, we have good news: there are several ways and tips that can help reduce fretting.</p>
<p></p>
<h2>1. Microdosing with psychedelics</h2>
<p>If you like to use innovative ways, you can try once to mull less by using magic mushrooms in microdoses. This involves taking psychedelics in a low dose over a few weeks. Large-scale research is still ongoing, yet this method is becoming increasingly popular among users. Initial studies also suggest that microdosing with psychedelics can have multiple benefits. For example, this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14512-3" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">study from 2022</a></span><span> identified </span>small to medium improvements in participants' mood and mental health. As a result, they may also engage in less mulling. Ruud shared<span> his </span>experiences microdosing <span style="color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../mushrooms/189-microdosing.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">magic mushrooms</a></span> in 2018<span> and </span>noticed that he mulled and mulled less.<br/><br/><strong>Microdosing:</strong><span> While </span>some users report positive experiences of reducing mulling thoughts through microdosing, it is important to note that the effects can be subjective and vary greatly from one individual to another. In addition, microdosing with psychedelics is illegal and unregulated in many countries, posing risks in terms of dosage, purity and safety. Moreover, using psychedelics can exacerbate psychological symptoms in people with a history of mental illnesses</p>
<p></p>
<h2>2. Mindfulness</h2>
<p>If you want a method to mull less that has been researched more, you can use mindfulness. This helps you focus on the here and now, making you worry less about the past and the future. Mindfulness can therefore make you fret less and, according to this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1809754" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2014 study</a></span>, it also<span> reduces </span>feelings of anxiety, stress and depression. By meditating for 10 to 20 minutes daily, you are already likely to mull less.</p>
<p></p>
<p><img alt="Mindfulness" src="../../../../../files/index.html@id=2566.jpg&amp;file=mindfullnes.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2>3. Mulling diary</h2>
<p>According to the experts at the MiekerSTOP Method, one of the best tips to reduce mulling<span> is </span>to use what is known as a mulling diary. For this, you can use an app or just a simple notebook. In it, you regularly write down your brooding thoughts and other worries. This way, you become aware of your thoughts. Moreover, the diary allows you to recognise patterns and possibly create strategies to reduce fretting. So using a diary can help reduce fretting and, according to this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jclp.22463" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2017 study</a></span>, it<span> can </span>also prevent or reduce depression.</p>
<p></p>
<h2>4. Supplements</h2>
<p>Did you know that taking supplements can be a good tip to combat fretting? Of course, not everyone reacts the same way to them, but some supplements do have this effect, according to scientists. Here are the best options:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Magnesium: </strong><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../article/1202444-marine-magnesium-together.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Magnesium</a></span><span> is </span>a supplement that can offer many benefits to users. For instance, it can also result in less fretting. Moreover, this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0180067" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2017 study</a></span><span> shows </span>that magnesium use is effective for moderate depression symptoms among adults.</li>
<li><strong>Ashwagandha: </strong><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../article/3203346-ashwaganda-10-ml.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Ashwagandha</a></span> has been<span> used </span>in alternative medicine for many years and can help combat fretting and stress. This <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3573577/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2012 study</a></span><span> suggests </span>that this supplement can reduce stress levels.</li>
<li><strong>L-theanine:<span> Less </span></strong>fretting is<span> also </span>possible with the help of L-theanine, according to this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11130-019-00771-5?crsi=662497574&amp;cicada_org_src=healthwebmagazine.com&amp;cicada_org_mdm=direct" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2019 scientific study</a></span>. This substance occurs naturally in green tea and is also in our <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="../../../../../article/2206525-brain-booster.html" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">Brain Booster</a></span>, among others.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supplements:</strong><span> The </span>efficacy of supplements such as magnesium, ashwagandha and L-theanine can vary from person to person. While some studies suggest positive effects, these are often small-scale and limited in duration. Supplements may interact with medications and cause side effects, especially with high doses or long-term use. It is therefore essential to consult a doctor or specialist beforehand.<br/><br/></p>

<h2>5. Exercise</h2>
<p>There is no doubt that exercise is good for your body. However, did you know that it can also be beneficial for your mental health? For example, this <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(18)30227-X/abstract" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2018 study</a></span><span> shows </span>that people who exercise have almost 50 per cent fewer days with poor mental health than those who do not exercise. This <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-1792-1_23" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">2018 study by Aaron Kandola and Brendon Stubbs</a></span><span> suggests </span>the same. These scientists mention that promoting exercise can be a method of preventing or treating anxiety disorders. So it can also help against brooding.<br/><br/>Worrying is part of life, but that doesn't mean you should just dwell on it. Fortunately, there are several ways to calm your thoughts. Whether you choose an innovative method such as microdosing, focus on the moment through mindfulness, write down your worries in a worry diary, support your body with supplements, or literally get moving - there is always an approach that suits you. Above all, try out what works for you, and remember: worrying less starts with awareness and action. You are not alone, and every step towards a calm mind is one step in the right direction.</p>
