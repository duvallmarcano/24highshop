<h2 id="understanding-ph">Understanding pH Levels in Cannabis Cultivation</h2>
<p>Potential hydrogen (pH) determines how acidic or alkaline a solution is. This is particularly important when discussing cannabis plants, as they require a very specific pH range to access the full range of macro- and micronutrients, as explained below.</p>
<h3>What Is Cannabis pH?</h3>
<p>pH levels in cannabis can refer to the pH of the water source, nutrient solution and soil. There must be the right pH level in the growing medium and nutrient solution for plants to grow and develop as healthily as possible.</p>
<h3>How pH Imbalance Causes Nutrient Lockout</h3>
<ul>
<li>If the water or nutrients are too acidic or alkaline, plants cannot access them.</li>
<li>Plants can become starved of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and trace elements.</li>
<li>pH imbalances can also cause nutrient toxicity.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Why Are Cannabis pH and EC So Important for Plant Health?</h3>
<p>There are two reasons why cannabis pH and EC are so important. First, the pH needs to remain within an optimal range. If it does not, the <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #1649f5;"><a href="../../seedshop/124-grow-supplies.html" style="color: #1649f5; text-decoration: underline;">nutrients</a></span> cannot be properly absorbed and used. Second, EC measures the strength or concentration of the nutrient solution.</p>
<h2 id="ideal-ph">Ideal pH for Hydro vs Soil Cannabis Growing</h2>
<p>If you are growing with organic soil or hydroponic systems, you will need to know the correct pH ranges for optimal nutrient uptake and availability.</p>
<h3>What Is the Best pH for Cannabis in Soil vs Hydro?</h3>
<p><img alt="Giving water to cannabis plant with the right EC and pH level nutrition" src="../../files/index.html@id=3124.jpg&amp;file=blogafbeelding.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<p><em>Photo 2 – EC and pH levels play a major role in nutrient accessibility.</em></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Soil:</strong> 6.0–7.0</li>
<li><strong>Hydro:</strong> 5.5–6.5</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to Check Soil and Hydro pH</h3>
<p>To check the pH level of cannabis growing in soil, you will need to use a soil <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #1649f5;"><a href="../../article/2202129-ph-meter-pro.html" style="color: #1649f5; text-decoration: underline;">pH meter</a></span>. This device is inserted into the soil and displays the current pH level.</p>
<p>In hydroponics, because you are growing in a soilless medium, the water in the nutrient solution reservoir needs to be checked and frequently monitored. This should be done using a digital pH pen for the most accurate results.</p>
<h3>Why Monitoring pH Is Crucial</h3>
<p>Without the correct pH range for soil or hydro, your plants will not be able to access much-needed nutrients. Imagine someone putting a lock on the fridge when you are hungry.</p>
<h2 id="nutrient-lockout">Preventing Nutrient Lockout from Incorrect pH</h2>
<p><img alt="This grower tests the EC level of a nutrient solution for a hydroponic system" src="../../files/index.html@id=3123.jpg&amp;file=Untitled.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<p><em>Photo 3 – This grower tests the EC level of a nutrient solution for a hydroponic system.</em></p>
<p>Nutrient lockout can be a major problem during the seedling, vegetative and especially flowering stages. Let us look at how to prevent nutrient lockout and maintain optimal pH levels.</p>
<h3>What Does Nutrient Lockout from Incorrect pH Mean?</h3>
<p>Nutrient lockout means that the plant’s roots cannot access or use the available nutrients. Essentially, the nutrients become unavailable, regardless of how much plant food is present.</p>
<h3>How to Prevent Nutrient Lockout Using Proper pH Control</h3>
<ol>
<li>Frequently monitor the pH of your nutrient solution and water source using a digital pH pen.</li>
<li>Use a soil pH meter to regularly check the pH of your growing medium.</li>
<li>Use pH-up or pH-down products when necessary, adding small amounts to the water until the desired pH is reached.</li>
<li>Always check the pH instead of estimating or second-guessing it.</li>
</ol>
<h3>Why Does Not Checking pH Affect Cannabis Nutrient Absorption?</h3>
<p>You would be surprised by how many growers do not check the pH levels of their water or nutrient solution. Simply mixing water and nutrients together and immediately feeding the plants is a habit many beginner growers develop. However, this can negatively affect cannabis nutrient absorption.</p>
<h2 id="ec-matters">Why EC Matters for Cannabis Growers</h2>
<p>You now know more about pH, so let us look at electrical conductivity, or EC. Optimal nutrient management allows you to grow healthy, robust plants capable of producing high yields. Below, we explain what EC is and why it matters.</p>
<h3>What Are Cannabis EC Levels?</h3>
<p>Electrical conductivity (EC) is a measurement used to determine how strong or weak a nutrient solution is. It is based on the amount of dissolved mineral salts a water source contains. The more nutrients present in the water, the higher the salt concentration and EC value.</p>
<p>EC is commonly measured in millisiemens per centimetre (mS/cm) or microsiemens per centimetre (µS/cm).</p>
<h3>How to Maintain Ideal EC Levels for Cannabis Plants</h3>
<p>Nutrient manufacturers usually provide recommended mixing ratios for their products. Using an <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #1649f5;"><a href="../../article/2202256-ec-meter-pro.html" style="color: #1649f5; text-decoration: underline;">EC meter</a></span> or TDS pen is the best way to check the actual concentration of the nutrient solution.</p>
<h3>Why EC Levels Matter for Cannabis Growth and Yield</h3>
<ul>
<li>Low EC levels can cause plants to become deficient in certain nutrients.</li>
<li>High EC levels can burn plants and cause nutrient toxicity.</li>
<li>The correct EC level encourages healthy growth and optimal yields.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="measuring-ec">Measuring EC Accurately</h2>
<p><img alt="Maintaining the correct EC levels from seed to harvest will help produce a healthy crop." src="../../files/index.html@id=3122.jpg&amp;file=bloggi.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<p><em>Photo 4 – Maintaining the correct EC levels from seed to harvest will help produce a healthy crop.</em></p>
<p>Measuring EC correctly allows you to adopt a professional and controlled approach to feeding your plants. The following sections explain the recommended EC levels for each growth stage and provide a step-by-step guide to measuring EC accurately.</p>
<h3>What Are the Correct EC Levels When Cannabis Is Growing?</h3>
<p>Below is a general guide to EC levels during the different stages of a cannabis plant’s lifecycle.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Seedling stage:</strong> 0.4–0.8 mS/cm (400–800 µS/cm)</li>
<li><strong>Vegetative stage:</strong> 0.8–1.8 mS/cm (800–1,800 µS/cm)</li>
<li><strong>Flowering stage:</strong> 1.8–2.0 mS/cm (1,800–2,000 µS/cm)</li>
</ul>
<h3>Step-by-Step Guide to Measuring EC</h3>
<ol>
<li>Calibrate your EC meter according to the manufacturer’s instructions.</li>
<li>Test your water source to establish a baseline reading and write the result down.</li>
<li>Add the nutrients to the water and stir thoroughly for 30–60 seconds.</li>
<li>Test the nutrient solution and write the result down.</li>
<li>Compare the baseline water reading with the nutrient solution reading.</li>
<li>Once the correct EC level has been reached, you can feed your plants.</li>
</ol>
<h3>Why Measuring EC Accurately Is Key</h3>
<p>Measuring EC is the most reliable way to determine the concentration of nutrients your plants are receiving. You can also test the runoff EC level to see whether it is higher or lower than the EC of the original nutrient solution.</p>
<p>High EC levels in the growing medium can result in overfertilisation, nutrient burn and salt buildup.</p>
<h2 id="runoff-ec">Reading Runoff EC in Cannabis Plants</h2>
<p>Knowing the EC level of the water draining from your pots can tell you a great deal about what is happening around the roots and how nutrients are being absorbed. The following sections explain what runoff is, why it is important and how to measure it.</p>
<h3>What Is Runoff EC in Cannabis and What Does It Tell You?</h3>
<p>Runoff is the water that drains from the bottom of the pots after watering. The EC of the runoff can provide useful information about the growing medium, nutrient accumulation and how much food the plants are using.</p>
<h3>How to Read Runoff EC in Cannabis Plants</h3>
<ol>
<li>Water the pot and collect a sample of the runoff in a clean plastic cup.</li>
<li>Place a calibrated EC pen into the runoff solution.</li>
<li>Compare the runoff EC with the EC of the original nutrient solution.</li>
<li>If the runoff EC is significantly higher, salts may be building up and the growing medium may need to be flushed.</li>
<li>If the runoff EC is significantly lower, the plants may be absorbing nutrients quickly and the feeding schedule may need to be adjusted.</li>
</ol>
<h3>Why Testing Runoff EC in Cannabis Soil and Hydro Systems Is Important</h3>
<p>Testing runoff can help determine whether you need to increase or decrease the EC, flush the growing medium or adjust how much plant food is provided during the vegetative and flowering stages.</p>
<p>Maintaining the correct EC and pH levels is especially important when using hydroponic systems. Plants grown hydroponically can suffer quickly when the EC level is too low or too high, as they have less buffering capacity than plants grown in soil.</p>
