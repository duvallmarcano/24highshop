<h2>What is a truffle ceremony?</h2>
<p>A truffle ceremony is a deliberately designed experience in which psilocybin truffles are used for introspection, emotional processing, or spiritual deepening <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a class="ref" href="preparing-truffle-ceremony-how-what-why-and-for-whom.html#bron-3" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">[3]</a></span>. The experience takes place in a calm environment and is often supported by intention work, music, and possibly guidance.</p>
<p>Psilocybin is converted in the body into psilocin, a substance that influences the serotonin 5-HT2A receptors. This temporarily alters communication between brain regions, which can lead to changed perception, intensified emotions, and profound insights.</p>
<h3>What does psilocybin do in the body?</h3>
<p>Psilocybin temporarily disrupts fixed patterns of thinking and perception, allowing new perspectives and emotional insights to become accessible.</p>
<h2>Why do people choose a truffle ceremony?</h2>
<p>People participate in a truffle ceremony for various reasons. Often it involves self-exploration, emotional processing, or spiritual deepening.</p>
<ul>
<li>Personal and spiritual development</li>
<li>Reflection on life questions or patterns</li>
<li>Breaking through entrenched thought processes</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Important:</strong><br/>A truffle ceremony is not a quick fix or a replacement for therapy, but an intensive process that requires preparation and integration. <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a aria-label="Source 1" class="ref" href="preparing-truffle-ceremony-how-what-why-and-for-whom.html#bron-1" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">[1]</a></span></p>
<p><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><img alt="group of people sharing their experiences truffle ceremony" src="../../files/index.html@id=2842.jpg&amp;file=ervaringen+delen+na+truffelceremonie.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></span></p>
<h2>Who is a truffle ceremony (not) suitable for?</h2>
<p>Not everyone is suitable to participate in a truffle ceremony. An honest self- or participant screening is essential for safety.</p>
<h3>Not recommended for:</h3>
<ol>
<li>A personal or family history of psychotic disorders</li>
<li>Severe, untreated depression or suicidal thoughts</li>
<li>Recent psychosis, manic episode, or severe panic attacks</li>
</ol>
<h3>Physical considerations</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular disease:</strong> temporary increase in heart rate and blood pressure</li>
<li><strong>Medication:</strong> possible interactions (e.g., MAO inhibitors, lithium)</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy and breastfeeding:</strong> use is discouraged</li>
</ul>
<h2>How do you prepare a truffle ceremony as safely as possible?</h2>
<h3>1. Set: mindset and intention</h3>
<p>The ‘set’ refers to your mental state, expectations, and intentions. It helps to formulate your intention in advance and have realistic expectations.</p>
<h3>2. Setting: environment and guidance</h3>
<p>A safe setting is calm, familiar, and low in stimuli. Many people choose a lying position, an eye mask, and music. An experienced trip sitter can provide additional safety.</p>
<h3>3. Dosage: start low (indicative)</h3>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> The ranges below are <em>indicative</em> and not personal advice. Effects vary by person, product, preparation, and setting. If in doubt, choose lower or postpone. <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a aria-label="Source 4" class="ref" href="preparing-truffle-ceremony-how-what-why-and-for-whom.html#bron-4" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">[4]</a></span></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>5–10 grams:</strong> low dose</li>
<li><strong>10–15 grams:</strong> medium dose</li>
<li><strong>&gt;15 grams:</strong> high, very intensive dose</li>
</ul>
<blockquote class="quote-block">
<p>Always start on the low end. A dose that is too high cannot be reversed.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>4. Harm reduction and integration</h3>
<ul>
<li>Do not combine psilocybin with alcohol or other drugs</li>
<li>Allow sufficient time afterward for rest and reflection</li>
<li>Never use psychedelics as a replacement for professional care</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where can you attend a truffle ceremony and what should you look for?</h2>
<p>Truffle ceremonies are offered in the Netherlands and elsewhere by various facilitators and organizations. They usually take place in a group setting or individually, under the guidance of a facilitator. Overviews of ceremonies and providers can be found online, but quality and professionalism vary greatly <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a class="ref" href="preparing-truffle-ceremony-how-what-why-and-for-whom.html#bron-5" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">[5]</a></span>. <br/><br/>This article does not recommend specific providers; use the checklist below to responsibly assess whether a setting suits you.</p>
<h3>What should you look for in a ceremony provider?</h3>
<p>Transparency about screening, preparation, guidance, and aftercare is essential.</p>
<ul>
<li>Is there prior screening for mental and physical health?</li>
<li>Is there attention to preparation <em>and</em> integration afterward?</li>
<li>Is the group size manageable?</li>
<li>Are risks and contraindications clearly discussed?</li>
</ul>
<p>Be cautious of providers who offer guarantees of healing or spiritual breakthroughs. Serious facilitators emphasize responsibility, safety, and personal boundaries.</p>
