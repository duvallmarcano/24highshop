<h2>Effects and side effects of kratom</h2>
<p>Is kratom really that safe? That remains to be seen. Poison centres in America received more than 3,400 reports related to kratom use between 2014 and 2019. These unfortunately included deaths. Kratom can therefore have side effects, which can be mild but also serious:</p>
<ul>
<li>Dry mouth</li>
<li>Nausea and vomiting</li>
<li>Constipation</li>
<li>Muscle pain</li>
<li>High blood pressure</li>
<li>Weight loss</li>
<li>Damage to the liver</li>
</ul>
<p>Kratom can also affect the nervous system and your mental well-being:</p>
<ul>
<li>Dizziness</li>
<li>Hallucinations</li>
<li>Drowsiness</li>
<li>Depression</li>
<li>Problems with breathing</li>
<li>Delusions</li>
<li>Confusion</li>
<li>Epileptic seizures</li>
<li>Tremours</li>
</ul>
<p>Some of these side effects can also be caused by dangerous contaminants in the drug, such as heavy metals or salmonella. Kratom by itself is probably not often life-threatening, but it can become so when combined with other drugs or medication.<br/><br/></p>

<h2>The addiction risk of kratom</h2>
<p>It is not yet clear whether kratom is actually addictive. It is known that some of the substances in kratom, especially mitragynine, can affect the opioid and dopamine receptors in the brain. These receptors are likely to be involved in the development of addiction.</p>
<p>In a <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/prescription-drug-abuse/in-depth/kratom/art-20402171" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">study</a></span> reported by the Mayo Clinic in the 18 June 2024 article "Kratom: Unsafe and ineffective", in which kratom was used as a treatment to quit opioids to reduce withdrawal symptoms, participants took the drug for 6 months. However, they reported withdrawal symptoms similar to those of opioid use. Users may start craving kratom. They may then need treatment for that again, as with buprenorphine and buprenorphine-naloxone.</p>
<p>Other research also seems to suggest that the active ingredient mitragynine in kratom can be addictive. In 2016, <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25262913/" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;">research</a></span> was<span> conducted </span>on rodents. The report titled Abuse potential and adverse cognitive effects of mitragynine (kratom) was published in Addiction Biology. The animals were administered kratom for 14 days. After 12 hours, they clearly experienced severe physical withdrawal symptoms and after 24 hours, an increase in anxiety was seen.</p>
<p>When you stop using kratom, you may also experience withdrawal symptoms, which can be mild but also severe, such as:</p>

<h2>Addicted to kratom?</h2>
<p>It is not always easy to see for yourself that you are addicted to a drug. Keep an eye on the following symptoms. Do you feel a desire to use more often? Do you also want to use at times that are not really appropriate for this? You may find that you often think or talk about kratom and hide your use from others. In fact, your life revolves around your kratom use. You may also be unable to function well without using the drug.<br/><br/></p>
<p>If you recognise some or even all of these points in yourself, chances are you are addicted. Don't hesitate and seek help as soon as possible. Take care of yourself. Your kratom use can undermine your health.</p>
<p></p>
<h2>The conclusion</h2>
<p>In fact, we do not yet know enough to say with certainty that kratom is safe. It does come with risks and there is a chance that it is addictive. Therefore, make sure you are aware of the risks. Realise that even though kratom is a natural product, that does not automatically mean it is safe. Stay cautious and keep an eye on your own well-being. Ask a doctor for advice if you have any doubts about your kratom use. If you are pregnant, do not use kratom either. The Mayo Clinic mentions that kratom is not safe for the unborn child and that the child may be born with withdrawal symptoms and need treatment.<br/><br/></p>
