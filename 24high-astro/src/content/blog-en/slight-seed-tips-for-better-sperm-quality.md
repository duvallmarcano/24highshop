<h2>When low sperm quality is a problem</h2>
<p>Fertility is a complex interplay of factors, both in men and women. In cases of unwanted childlessness, the woman is often looked at, while in about half the cases, the cause lies with the man. A common reason is reduced sperm quality, such as low sperm count or sperm of insufficient quality. A healthy <span>sperm <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #100af6;"><span><a href="https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/low-sperm-count/symptoms-causes/syc-20374585" style="color: #100af6; text-decoration: underline;">count</a></span></span> </span>is above 15 million per millilitre of semen, with at least 4% of the sperm cells needing to be normally formed and 32% motile. These characteristics cannot be seen without a medical examination. It is therefore important to consult a doctor when in doubt. A simple semen analysis can provide insight into the quality and quantity of sperm.<br/><br/><img src="../../files/index.html@id=2338.jpg&amp;file=sperm.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>

<p>Smoking, excessive alcohol consumption and recreational drugs negatively affect sperm.</p>

<h3>Weight:</h3>
<p>Being overweight affects hormone balance, which can negatively affect sperm production.</p>
<h3>Medical conditions:</h3>
<p>Infections (such as STDs), testicular abnormalities and problems with ejaculation can contribute to reduced fertility.</p>
<h3>Environmental factors:</h3>
<p>Exposure to heavy metals, chemicals, high temperatures and radiation can harm sperm.</p>
<h3>Clothing:</h3>
<p>Tight clothing or underwear can increase the temperature around the testicles, which can affect sperm quality.</p>
<h3>Diet:</h3>
<p>A diet low in vegetables, fruits and essential nutrients can lead to a lack of vitamins and minerals important for sperm.<br/><br/><img src="../../files/index.html@id=2339.jpg&amp;file=sperma.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2>Tips for better sperm quality</h2>
<p>A healthy lifestyle can help improve sperm quality. Here are some practical tips:</p>
<ol>
<li>
<p><strong>Lifestyle changes:</strong><br/>Stop smoking, limit alcohol and avoid drug use.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Healthy weight:</strong><br/>Aim for a healthy BMI and exercise regularly.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Stress management:</strong><br/>Reduce stress through relaxation techniques, meditation or adequate rest.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Healthy diet:</strong><br/>Ensure a diet rich in vegetables, fruits and essential nutrients, such as zinc and folic acid, are important. Supplementation can be helpful, but consult a doctor or dietician for this.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Clothing:</strong><br/>Choose loose-fitting underwear and avoid prolonged exposure to heat (such as saunas and hot baths). <span>Tight underwear and tight trousers can <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #100af6;"><span><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30102388/" style="color: #100af6; text-decoration: underline;">cause</a></span></span> worse semen. </span></p>
