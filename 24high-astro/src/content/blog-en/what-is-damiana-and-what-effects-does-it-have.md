<h2>Damiana: origin and history</h2>
<p><span style="color: #1621d2;"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnera_diffusa" style="color: #1621d2; text-decoration: underline;">Damiana</a></span> (Tunera diffusa or Tunera Aphrodisiaca in Latin) is a flowering shrub that grows in Central and South America and the Caribbean. Of this, its leaves are used for medicinal purposes. For instance, 100 years ago, its traditional use was already published by the Mayans, who used it for dizziness and balance disorders. Traditionally, it has been known as a powerful aphrodisiac. In other words, a way to boost your libido and potency. This is also how it was promoted when it was marketed in the United States back in 1874.<br/><br/><img alt="maya" src="../../files/index.html@id=1949.jpg&amp;file=maya.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><br/></img></p>
<h2>The effects of damiana: how healthy is it?</h2>
<p>Damiana has several potential effects, some of which have been known for centuries. Below is an overview of its main effects.</p>
<h3>Wide range of uses</h3>
<p>Traditionally, damiana leaves are used not only as an aphrodisiac, but also for a variety of other ailments such as depression, gastrointestinal, bladder and kidney problems and menstrual complaints. It can also lower blood sugar levels.</p>
<p>As written earlier in this article, damiana is full of so-called flavonoids. These are antioxidants: super healthy substances found in fruits and vegetables, among others. They help clean up free radicals, which are substances that can actually cause damage to your cells and body.</p>

A drop in your testosterone levels, for instance, causes a lower sex drive.</p>

<h3>Relaxation</h3>
<p>Besides improving libido, damiana can also induce <span style="color: #1621d2;"><a href="../../smartshop/35-relaxation.html" style="color: #1621d2; text-decoration: underline;">relaxation</a></span>. It can therefore help reduce anxiety and stress. For example, a study showed that damiana extract reduced anxiety levels in mice. This is probably due to one of the <span style="color: #1621d2;"><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24468305/" style="color: #1621d2; text-decoration: underline;">22</a></span> flavonoids it contains, called apigenin. This compound is known to reduce anxiety and stabilise hormones. In a <span style="color: #1621d2;"><a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3040867/#CIT4" style="color: #1621d2; text-decoration: underline;">study</a></span> with mice exposed to stress, this substance even had a similar effect to diazepam - a strong prescription-only sedative. Apigenin, by the way, can be found in all kinds of plants. For example, in camomile, which is also known to calm people down.</p>
