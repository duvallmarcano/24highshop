Although they are often mentioned together, they differ fundamentally in their effects, legal status, and applications.</p>
<p>This article offers a science-based comparison between CBD and THC to help you make more informed decisions when using cannabis-related products.<br/><br/></p>
<h2><span style="color: #3eae16;">What Are Cannabinoids, and How Do They Work?</span></h2>
<p>The cannabis plant (<em>Cannabis sativa L.</em>) contains over 100 bioactive substances, known as cannabinoids. The two most well-known are:</p>
<ul>
<li>THC (<em>delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol</em>)</li>
<li>CBD (<em>cannabidiol</em>)</li>
</ul>
<p>Both compounds interact with the endocannabinoid system—a complex receptor network involved in pain perception, mood, appetite, and sleep regulation.</p>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms19030833" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">Zou &amp; Kumar (2018), International Journal of Molecular Sciences</a></span></p>
<p><img alt="cannabis thc" src="../../../../../files/index.html@id=923.jpg&amp;file=cbdcannabisweed.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><br/></img></p>
<h2><span style="color: #3eae16;">CBD Is Non-Psychoactive, THC Is Not</span></h2>
<p>The primary distinction between the two lies in their effect on the brain:</p>
<ul>
<li>THC activates CB1 receptors in the brain, leading to a "high" feeling and affecting memory, coordination, and perception.</li>
<li>CBD is non-psychoactive and may even mitigate THC’s effects. Many users report relaxation without altered consciousness.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Study:</strong> <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s13311-015-0387-1" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">Blessing et al. (2015), Neurotherapeutics</a><br/><br/></span></p>

<h2><span style="color: #3eae16;">Different Plant Sources</span></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>CBD</strong> is extracted from hemp, which naturally contains high CBD and low THC.</li>
<li><strong>THC</strong> is derived from marijuana, selectively bred for high THC content.</li>
</ul>
<p><img src="../../../../../files/index.html@id=922.jpg&amp;file=medicinalewiet.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<h2><span style="color: #3eae16;">Applications and Use</span></h2>
<h3>CBD: General Wellness</h3>
<p>CBD is widely used as a supplement and available in oils, capsules, teas, creams, and skincare. Potential uses include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stress and anxiety relief</li>
<li>Improved sleep</li>
<li>Muscle and joint support</li>
<li>Skin issues (e.g., eczema or acne)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> Most health claims are not yet approved by the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA).</p>
<h3>THC: Medicinal Use Only</h3>
<p>THC is available by prescription and may be used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Chronic pain management</li>
<li>Muscle spasms (e.g., MS)</li>
<li>Nausea due to chemotherapy</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> <span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #0907f5;"><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659074/" rel="noopener" style="color: #0907f5; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">Pubmed</a></span></p>
<p>CBD and THC both belong to the so-called ‘cannabinoids’. Although <span style="text-decoration: underline;"><span style="color: #2301fc; text-decoration: underline;"><a href="https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/cannabidiol-cbd-what-we-know-and-what-we-dont-2018082414476" style="color: #2301fc; text-decoration: underline;">CBD</a></span></span> and THC both come from cannabis, these two substances are very different from each other.
