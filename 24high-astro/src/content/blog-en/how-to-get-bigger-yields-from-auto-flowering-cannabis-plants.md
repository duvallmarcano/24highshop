<h2 id="increase-yields">How to Increase Autoflower Yields Successfully</h2>
<p>When your goal is to increase autoflower yields, there are several ways to boost them. Light intensity plays a direct role in photosynthesis and plant growth <span style="color: #1649f5;"><a href="how-to-get-bigger-yields-from-auto-flowering-cannabis-plants.html#bron1" style="color: #1649f5;">[1]</a></span>.</p>
<h3>What helps increase autoflower yields the most?</h3>
<ul>
<li>Intense lighting makes a significant difference in yield</li>
<li>The right amount of nutrients from seed to harvest</li>
<li>Optimal growing temperatures <span style="color: #1649f5;"><a href="how-to-get-bigger-yields-from-auto-flowering-cannabis-plants.html#bron4" style="color: #1649f5;">[4]</a></span></li>
<li>Humidity levels kept within the correct range</li>
<li>Proper pH management and maintenance <span style="color: #1649f5;"><a href="how-to-get-bigger-yields-from-auto-flowering-cannabis-plants.html#bron3" style="color: #1649f5;">[3]</a></span></li>
<li>Plant training techniques applied up to week 5</li>
<li>Soil-grown crops require a pH level of 6.0–7.0</li>
<li>Hydro-grown crops require a pH level of 5.5–6.5</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to Increase Autoflower Yields Indoors</h3>
<p>Indoors, you can create the ideal growing environment for autos and provide ample photosynthetically active radiation (PAR). Many growers prefer to give indoor autoflowering plants 18–20 hours of light to help maximise yields.</p>
<h3>Why Increasing Autoflower Yields Requires a Different Approach</h3>
<p>Unlike photoperiod plants, which offer the luxury of long vegetative periods, autos are genetically programmed to flower after around 4 weeks. This means that when you include the 14-day seedling stage, autos only have around 2 weeks of true vegetative growth. Regardless of the number of light hours, buds will begin to form soon after.</p>
<h2 id="high-yield-strains">High-Yielding Autoflower Strains to Maximise Production</h2>
<p><img alt="Large cannabis flower with purple leaves and dense buds, photographed against a black background." src="../../files/index.html@id=2998.jpg&amp;file=resized_1920x1080_padded.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"><br/><em>Picture - Large-yielding autos result in bigger-yielding plants - Credit: Seed Stockers</em></img></p>
<p>One of the best ways to increase autoflower yields is to start with high-yielding genetics. Not only do these autos grow bigger and wider, producing more in the process, but they are also ideal for growers with limited space who want the highest yield possible.</p>
<h3>What are the 4 best high-yielding autoflower strains?</h3>
<ul>
<li><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #1649f5;"><a href="../../article/0204483-amnesia-auto-vision-seeds-3-seeds.html" style="color: #1649f5; text-decoration: underline;">Amnesia Auto (Vision Seeds)</a></span> – Known for vigorous growth and generous resin production, with strong yields compared to many other autoflowers.</li>
<li><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #1649f5;"><a href="../../article/8102858-sweet-skunk-automatic-3-seeds-rqs.html" style="color: #1649f5; text-decoration: underline;">Sweet Skunk Automatic (Royal Queen Seeds)</a></span> – A reliable producer with yields around 400–450 g/m² under good conditions.</li>
<li><span style="text-decoration: underline; color: #1649f5;"><a href="../../article/0204595-vision-jack-auto-vision-seeds-feminized-3-seeds.html" style="color: #1649f5; text-decoration: underline;">Vision Jack Auto (Vision Seeds)</a></span> – Stable genetics with yields of up to around 400 g/m² indoors.</li>
<li><a href="../../article/8102802-northern-light-automatic-3-seeds-rq.html">Northern Lights Automatic</a> – Classic autoflower genetics known for above-average yields and reliable performance.</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to Choose High-Yielding Autoflower Strains for Your Setup</h3>
<p>When choosing the best autoflowering strain for your setup, consider the following:</p>
<ul>
<li>How many grams per square metre does the strain yield?</li>
<li>How many weeks will it take to harvest the strain from start to finish?</li>
<li>Does the strain have a strong reputation for producing large yields?</li>
<li>Is it high-maintenance or easy to grow?</li>
<li>Is it well suited to beginner growers?</li>
</ul>
<h3>Why Genetics Play a Key Role in Autoflower Yields</h3>
<p>There is a huge variety of cannabis genetics available. When it comes to achieving higher yields and better overall performance, genetics make a massive difference.</p>
<h2 id="training-autos">Training Autoflowers to Increase Yield</h2>
<p><em><img alt="Cannabis plant in a pot with young green leaves, placed in an indoor growing environment with a mesh background." src="../../files/index.html@id=2995.jpg&amp;file=resized_1920x1080_padded_3.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></em><br/><em>Picture - This auto has been tied down on day 21 - Credit: Stoney Tark</em></p>
<p>Training your autoflowering cannabis plants can help improve their shape, structure, and yield. With such a short window before flowering begins, however, training autoflowers can be quite tricky.</p>
<h3>What is training autoflowers, and which methods work best?</h3>
<p>There are several plant training techniques you can apply to autos to boost yields.</p>
<ul>
<li>Topping: Remove the top crown shoot to create two or more top colas</li>
<li>Fimming: Cutting away around 75% of the tip to promote lower growth</li>
<li>Supercropping: Bending or snapping the stem or side branch to encourage stronger growth</li>
<li>LST: A low-stress technique that involves tying the top node downwards</li>
<li>Pruning: Removing fan leaves and axillary shoots below a certain node</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to Train Autoflowers Without Stunting Growth</h3>
<p>The best advice I can give is to avoid overdoing the training, allow enough recovery time, and stay away from high-stress techniques such as topping, fimming, and supercropping after week 4.</p>
<h3>Why Training Autoflowers Can Boost Bud Production</h3>
<p>A simple bit of plant training performed at the right time can increase autoflower yields. I have trained cannabis plants for the last 20 years, and I can say with confidence that autos respond well to gentle training.</p>
<ul>
<li>Make sure you give plants the right amount of recovery time between training sessions</li>
<li>Some autos are better suited to plant training than others</li>
<li>Training should ideally be done between weeks 3 and 4</li>
<li>Training once flowering starts can cause stunted growth</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="feeding-autos">Feeding Autos for Bigger Buds and Higher Yields</h2>
<p>Nutrients are essential to a cannabis plant's growth, development, and yield potential <span style="color: #1649f5;"><a href="how-to-get-bigger-yields-from-auto-flowering-cannabis-plants.html#bron2" style="color: #1649f5;">[2]</a></span>. <span>You do not want to give plants too little or too much nutrition, so finding the right balance is key to increasing autoflower yields.</span></p>
<h3>What is the Best Approach to Feeding Autos?</h3>
<p>Less is more. Because autos have a short life cycle, usually ranging between 10 and 14 weeks from seed to harvest, you need to be especially attentive.</p>
<ul>
<li>Autos should be fed a high-nitrogen feed during the first 4 weeks</li>
<li>After week 5, you should feed autos higher levels of phosphorus (P) and potassium (K)</li>
<li>Nutrient brands usually offer separate grow and bloom nutrients</li>
<li>Always follow the nutrient brand’s mixing ratio guide</li>
<li>Avoid using over-fertilised soil for starting autoflowering seedlings</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to Feed Autoflowers for Bigger Yields</h3>
<p>You should make sure your autoflowering plants have enough nutrients to support their full life cycle. You can use slow-release organic matter, such as enriched soil or compost, and supplement it with organic or synthetic liquid nutrients for faster uptake.</p>
<h3>Why Proper Feeding Improves Bud Size and Quality</h3>
<p>Underfed plants will show signs of nutrient deficiencies and may appear weak, brittle, and slow-growing. Overfertilised plants may struggle to produce top-quality buds, and flower development can be significantly affected. Either way, the yield will suffer, which is why proper feeding is extremely important.</p>
<h2 id="lighting-schedule">Autoflower Lighting Schedule for Maximum Yield</h2>
<p><img alt="Close-up of a cannabis bud with many trichomes and orange hairs on a black background." src="../../files/index.html@id=2997.png&amp;file=resized_1920x1080_padded_4.png" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/><br/><em>Picture - An example of a high-yielding auto grown using 20 hours of light each day - Credit: Seed Stockers</em></p>
<p>If you really want to increase autoflower yields, then be prepared to grow autos indoors. Below, I explain the best lighting schedule, how to optimise it, and why lighting schedules impact autos:</p>
<div style="display: flex; gap: 30px; flex-wrap: wrap; margin-top: 20px;">
<div style="flex: 1; min-width: 260px; background: #f5f7ff; padding: 20px; border-radius: 12px;">
<h4 style="margin-top: 0;">What is the best indoor lighting schedule for autoflowers?</h4>
<ul>
<li>Autos grown indoors can be kept on 18–20 hours of light per day from seed to harvest</li>
<li>24 hours of light can work well; however, it may produce shorter internodal spacing</li>
<li>The 12/12 light cycle is not recommended for growers aiming for large yields</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div style="flex: 1; min-width: 260px; background: #fff8f2; padding: 20px; border-radius: 12px;">
<h4 style="margin-top: 0;">How to Optimise Your Autoflower Lighting Schedule Indoors</h4>
<ul>
<li>Keep the light cycle set to 18–20 hours per day</li>
<li>Plant new seedlings every 4 weeks for a perpetual harvest</li>
<li>18–20 hours of light will also reduce electricity bills compared to 24/0</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<h3>Why Lighting Schedules Impact Autoflower Yield</h3>
<p>Although autos can flower regardless of the light cycle, more light generally means better results. High-yielding autoflower strains thrive under intense lighting, especially when combined with proper nutrients and a little plant training.</p>
<h2 id="outdoor-yields">How to Grow High-Yielding Autoflower Cannabis Outdoors</h2>
<p><img alt="Detailed macro photograph of a cannabis flower with crystal resin and orange pistils on a black background." src="../../files/index.html@id=2994.jpg&amp;file=resized_1920x1080_padded_2.jpg" style="width: 1100px; max-width: 100%;"/></p>
<p>Planting autos outdoors is a low-cost, exciting, and discreet way to cultivate cannabis. Humidity plays an important role in plant transpiration and overall growth balance <a href="how-to-get-bigger-yields-from-auto-flowering-cannabis-plants.html#bron5">[5]</a>.</p>
<h3>Why the Right Climate and Environment Can Increase Autoflower Yields</h3>
<p><span>Cannabis plants require a specific climate and environment to thrive and deliver the large yields many growers aim for. Autos are tough, resilient, and sturdy plants adapted to colder climates and short summers. The more sun you have, the better. However, many autoflowering strains are reliable performers even in more challenging climates.</span></p>
